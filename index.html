Tulsa and Oklahoma

A quiet Saturday at City Hall and the Capitol. The Oklahoma State Election Board’s results page still labels the Nov. 3 general unofficial. County boards were due to certify after 5 p.m. Friday; state and federal races are scheduled for certification Tuesday, Nov. 10. No contest or recount filing turned up in today’s sweep, and the board had not posted certified statewide totals by this evening. No rebuild of Tuesday’s ballot here.

National / foreign

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations weekly overview (No. 14, produced Nov. 6, covering through Nov. 4) is the deed, not another speech about talks. It logged 11 attacks on commercial shipping in the last reporting week — eight in the Strait of Hormuz and three in the Arabian Gulf. Three of those vessels sank, five needed assistance, and three continued their voyages. Combined Hormuz transits were 170 outbound and 155 inbound, up from 122 outbound a week earlier (inbound was unchanged at 155), and still about 65 percent below the pre-conflict average. AIS-visible transits rose to 71 from 64; UKMTO says U.S.-stated facilitated transits were roughly level and cannot be independently verified from AIS. About 80 percent of transits remain energy carriers, per U.S. naval reporting as cited by UKMTO. Since July 6, 63 of 88 projectile strikes it counted were on the southern route — the Oman-side lane dark tankers still use. Year-to-date in its voluntary reporting area: 166 warnings, 22 advisories, and 155 Iran–U.S. conflict-related incidents. Thursday night’s unnamed outbound tanker explosion east of Khasab (Warning 197-26) falls after this weekly’s Nov. 4 cutoff.

Al Jazeera, reporting from Tehran, said two projectiles hit Iran’s Qeshm Island on Friday night and quoted President Trump calling the strikes “minor.” That is reporting plus a presidential word, not a CENTCOM release opened here. Iran’s navy said Saturday that a tanker running without a transponder caught fire in the eastern strait after hitting a mine; Washington has said the mines are cleared. Treat that as an Iranian claim, and do not fold it into Warning 197-26 (an attack-labeled waterline explosion; that ship kept its own power). Separately, IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi told reporters the force’s long-standing roughly 2,000-kilometer missile-range cap “has been lifted” — words, not a test. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said he spoke with 12 counterparts in 48 hours. The incentive: paper oil sold off last week on ceasefire talk while the weekly hull count shows the waterway is still being shot at. The political cost of expensive fuel was already paid at the midterms.

Bitcoin

Two Treasury deeds landed Friday, and they pull in opposite directions.

FinCEN, as part of “Operation Economic Outcast,” issued an order cutting the offshore exchange MEXC Global off from the U.S. financial system. Covered U.S. institutions will be prohibited from processing funds transfers to or from the platform under section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act. The order takes effect 30 days after it is published in the Federal Register. Treasury’s claim — not a court finding — is that from May 2021 to May 2026 the exchange moved at least $16 billion tied to sanctions evasion and cybercrime, including about $1 billion with identified Iranian virtual-currency exchangers and about $22 million connected to the Central Bank of Iran and the IRGC-Qods Force, plus about $1.5 billion linked to Russian sanctions evasion. Secretary Scott Bessent framed it as a binary: support the Iranian regime, or keep access to dollar rails. Wartime sanctions enforcement is the incentive; a large exchange is the target.

The same day, FinCEN and the Federal Reserve withdrew their October 27, 2020 proposal that would have lowered the cross-border Recordkeeping and Travel Rule threshold from $3,000 to $250 and spelled out that those rules cover convertible virtual currency and legal-tender digital assets. The withdrawal notice is on public inspection (filed Nov. 6, scheduled to publish Nov. 9); the agencies say they will not finish that notice of proposed rulemaking. The existing $3,000 rules stay. Their stated reasons: more than five years have passed and they want fresh comment before any similar rewrite, and the crypto market has changed (they cite the 2025 GENIUS Act). This is a withdrawn expansion of surveillance, not a repeal of the current travel rule.

Flows matched the risk-off week. Farside’s table for U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs: Friday, Nov. 6, net −$411.9 million, almost all of it BlackRock’s IBIT (−$403.0 million); Fidelity’s FBTC took in $42.8 million. For the week (Nov. 2–6): −$398.6 million, −$115.6 million, +$74.6 million, −$430.2 million, and −$411.9 million — about $1.28 billion out, the worst week on that tape since late June. Spot is still hanging around $76,000. No protocol change.

Sports

Oklahoma State beat Kansas State 66–28 in Manhattan at noon. The Cowboys (6–3, 4–2 Big 12) led 49–14 at half and scored the most points in the 78-game series. Drew Mestemaker went 22-of-28 for 313 yards and four touchdown passes, plus a rushing score and a receiving score; Caleb Hawkins ran for 175 yards and three touchdowns and crossed 1,000 rushing yards on the season (1,081). LaDainian Fields intercepted two more passes, his sixth and seventh of the year. Next: Texas Tech at Boone Pickens on Nov. 14. OU plays at Florida tonight (6:45 p.m. CT). OSU wrestling’s first dual is Drexel at Gallagher-Iba on Sunday. Cardinals and Braves remain offseason quiet.
