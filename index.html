Stitt won't tell the attorney general what his lawyers cost

Records (The Frontier, from state spending data at OMES): Gov. Kevin Stitt’s office spent more than $3.1 million from February 2020 through June 2026 on outside lawyers for fights with tribes and challenges to the McGirt decision. About $1.3 million went to one Oklahoma City firm, Ryan Whaley, which billed up to $400 an hour on the gaming-compact cases. On the record (AG’s office): state law requires executive-branch officials to send the attorney general copies of outside-counsel contracts, with the scope of the work and estimated cost. Stitt’s office sent none in 2020, 2021, 2024 or 2025, and gave no reason. Stitt’s office points to a 2025 Oklahoma Supreme Court ruling that he can hire his own lawyers without the AG’s approval. That ruling covered permission, though, not the reporting requirement. The incentive: Stitt and Attorney General Drummond have been feuding for years, and Stitt leaves office in January. Taxpayers are paying the bills either way and can’t easily see the total.

Stitt and Vance trade words on immigration

Stitt is calling for a “bipartisan immigration reform” effort and says it isn’t amnesty. He wants visas tied to state workforce needs, stronger enforcement, and a role for states in vouching for workers (KOKH). Vance had pushed back on X and asked what the phrase means. There’s no bill and no vote, so for now it’s just positioning.

Hern takes the Senate seat Monday

Kevin Hern is sworn in Nov. 30 and replaces appointed Sen. Alan Armstrong in the seat Markwayne Mullin left to become Homeland Security secretary (Oklahoma Voice).

National and foreign

Iran says two more tankers hit its mines. Nobody else has confirmed it. Claim (IRGC, via Tasnim and Reuters): two tankers trying an “unauthorized” crossing of the Strait of Hormuz hit mines north of Khasab, Oman. Iran says one was a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels that was left burning, and that the ships had their transponders off and U.S. fighter cover. Confirmed (Joint Maritime Information Center, Update 117, as of 1300 UTC Thursday): four projectile attacks on tankers in the strait in the past four days, the threat level stays “severe,” and there’s no mine strike on its list. The UAE’s ADNOC has also barred tankers carrying crude, fuel or chemicals from its waters unless they’re calling at its own ports. The incentive: Tehran wants shippers to believe its “restricted zone” is enforced, whether or not each claimed hit happened. Brent settled at $96.57, up 1.2%, and WTI had no settlement because of the holiday (Reuters).

OPEC+ meets Sunday on 2027 output

Delegates told Bloomberg the group is leaning toward keeping current quotas through January, and that members are arguing over a capacity audit. The group is working without the UAE now, and Iraq and Kazakhstan want their capacity estimates raised so they can pump more (Reuters). That’s reporting, not a decision. Watch what they actually set on Nov. 29.

Nvidia: pardon talk ahead of next week's testimony

On the record (quotes as reported by ABC News): Trump told the New York Post a pardon for Jensen Huang is “always an option, I guess, but I don’t think it will be necessary.” Vance said Huang doesn’t “need a pardon at all” and that he hasn’t seen evidence of a crime. Confirmed (House China Select Committee): Huang sits for a closed-door transcribed interview Tuesday, Dec. 1, and Nvidia’s general counsel Tim Teter on Dec. 2. Reported (Bloomberg): Huang testifies publicly before Senate Banking on Dec. 3. Why it matters: this is the most export-controlled chipmaker in America, and its CEO was never formally investigated. Raising a pardon before anyone has been charged tells you how the White House sees its own exposure. Next week’s testimony is the first time he answers questions on the record. FAA: still no official root cause, 13 days on.

Economy and markets

U.S. markets were closed for Thanksgiving and close early Friday. Gold slipped to about $3,564, after briefly falling below $3,500 Wednesday for the first time since September 2025. Silver is about $41.47 (Reuters via Kitco). The reason is the Fed: after Wednesday’s hot PCE number, markets expect a rate hike in December and another early next year. Higher rates make non-yielding metal less attractive, even with inflation running at 3.8%.

OU (7–4) plays at Missouri Saturday at 3:15 p.m. CT, and Oklahoma State (7–4) hosts Kansas at 5:15. Happy Thanksgiving.
