Peak on the Parkway TIF hearing is tomorrow

The Tulsa City Council’s Wednesday 5 p.m. agenda (filed 11/13) has the first public hearing on the Peak on the Parkway project plan. It would create four new tax-increment districts: three capture both property and sales tax, one captures property tax only, and the Tulsa Authority for Economic Opportunity runs the money. The second hearing is Dec. 2. Same agenda: a $65,000 judgment payment from the sinking fund in Moore v. City of Tulsa. A TIF means future tax growth gets pledged to the project instead of going to the general fund. Watch who’s in the room and what the increment buys.

Broken Arrow extends its data-center pause to June 30, 2027

The council voted to keep the moratorium on new data-center applications and hired a consultant to write zoning rules, with water and infrastructure load named as the concerns (News On 6). That’s a real local check on the large-load buildout while PSO and SPP plan for it.

TPS bond-office kickback case moves to arraignment Nov. 30

Former bond director Chris Hudgins and two contractors waived preliminary hearing on 27 felony counts. The DA and AG allege TPS paid for roofing at 16 campuses that was never done (Public Radio Tulsa). The State Auditor’s August audit found 10 of 54 HVAC projects from 2019–2025 didn’t go to the low bidder. This is voter-approved bond money, so it’s a citywide story, not school-board noise.

Downtown

ground broke on a $7M Center of the Universe rebuild. Bank of Oklahoma is the lead donor, it gets renamed BOK Plaza, and completion is targeted for fall 2027. Grand jury petition: organizers say they have the 5,000 signatures for a citizens’ grand jury on DA Steve Kunzweiler, including juvenile-detention conditions. A judge still has to verify the signatures, so it’s not certified yet.

State

The House swore in 25 freshmen Tuesday, the biggest class since 2018. Democrats gained six seats, but the GOP supermajority holds. Organizational day is Jan. 5, and session opens Feb. 1 with Gov.-elect Mazzei’s first State of the State (Oklahoma Voice). Rep. Danny Williams (R-Seminole) took the oath after winning with over 60% despite a House committee finding that he harassed staff and recommending he resign. Ethics Commission and AG probes are still open.

A federal judge (Kelly, D.D.C.) threw out the Cherokee, Chickasaw, Choctaw and Citizen Potawatomi challenge to Stitt’s 2020 Comanche and Otoe-Missouria compacts for lack of standing. He didn’t rule on the merits, and the compacts still permit sports betting and house-banked games that state law forbids (Journal Record). Stitt also wants AG Drummond to pay $56K in legal fees after Drummond dropped his defamation suit (Oklahoman, paywalled, headline only).

Iran–US exchange resumes after a two-month lull

Iran launched a third round of drones at a U.S. radar site in Bahrain early Tuesday. CENTCOM says all were intercepted with no damage or injuries. U.S. jets then struck an IRGC missile-launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, which a U.S. official called self-defense (AP, CBS). VP Vance said Washington won’t let it become a prolonged war. Here’s the action-versus-words gap: tanker tracking shows Hormuz crude transits back near pre-war levels (~13.5M bpd, per DTN), and oil barely moved. Markets are treating this as a contained exchange so far. That’s what traders are pricing, not a fact.

The FAA wants $50.1B. Congress wants to know where the first $12.5B went. DOT formally asked Congress for $50.1B to finish air traffic control modernization. Senators from both parties pushed back. DOT says all of the 2025 money is allocated, while reports put actual spending around $4B (Axios, AP). The incentive is plain: a crisis week is the best time to ask for money. Lawmakers are right to ask for the receipts first.

FAA ground stop: no root cause yet, router installs paused

Confirmed (FAA Q&A, FAA spokesman Ian Gregor): new Juniper router installations are paused, the broadcast function is disabled on 60+ routers already installed, and the network is running on Cisco gear about 25 years old. The two suspect routers (El Dorado, AR, and Amarillo, TX) are being tested at L3Harris in Florida. Reported (Washington Post, one source close to the probe): an FAA official supervised the El Dorado cutover from copper to fiber, tested it, and signed off eight minutes before the network began to fail. Still unknown: why the router flooded the network. Senators asked for a joint FAA, L3Harris and Juniper briefing. Why it matters: if the Post report holds, the FAA’s own sign-off is in the failure chain, not just a contractor. That changes who gets blamed, and it bears on the $50B ask.

NSA classified network degraded

Confirmed (NSA statement, 11/17): “temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance.” Some internal systems were affected, and the agency says mission-critical capabilities stayed up. Reported (Washington Post, AP, NBC, citing officials): the outage hit NSA Net, the internal classified messaging system, and staff arrivals were staggered. No indication of an attack. Speculation: none worth repeating. Why it matters: this is the fourth big outage at a critical system in a week (FAA, Microsoft 365, Chase, now NSA), and CNBC says traders cited it in Tuesday’s selloff. So far every official cause is the same: internal change or maintenance, not attackers. That’s a pattern of fragile systems, not a coordinated campaign. Watch for any change to that story.

Nvidia / Megaspeed: DOJ confirms a criminal probe

Confirmed (joint Commerce–DOJ statement, 11/17): Commerce opened a Megaspeed export-control investigation in 2024, requested Nvidia’s sales records in May 2025, and did an on-site check in Singapore with local police in June 2025. Nvidia put Megaspeed on its no-sell list that same June. DOJ now leads a criminal investigation. Nobody has been charged. Confirmed (Nvidia): the board won’t reinstate Jensen Huang. Reported (WSJ): Heidrick & Struggles is running the CEO search. Read the incentive: the statement’s real job is to answer “inaccurate information” and claim credit for Lutnick and Blanche.

Closed

Chase’s consumer-banking slowdown. The bank said it was a software bug, not cyber, and transactions were back to normal Monday evening (AP). Microsoft 365: nothing new until the final post-incident report, due Friday.

The Fed is hiking, and stocks are breaking down

Markets have a 25 bp December hike priced in, from 3.75–4.00%. Tuesday, NY Fed’s Williams said more hikes are likely but argued against a half-point, and Cleveland’s Hammack wants “slow, steady” hikes (Reuters, Bloomberg). On the data: October retail sales rose 0.2% (Census), and the Fed’s own release shows industrial production down 0.4%. Utilities fell 4.3% after a hot September, mining fell 0.8%, and manufacturing rose 0.3%. Capacity utilization is 76.4%, three points under its long-run average. Rate hikes into a soft factory floor is the late-cycle setup to watch.

Nvidia after the bell

Q3 revenue was $115.8B, up 103% year over year. Data center was $107.6B, and net income $65.3B on a 74.8% gross margin. Guidance is $130B (±3%) with zero China data-center revenue assumed (Nvidia release). The stock was up about 4% after hours. The AI spending boom is real in the invoices. Whether customers ever earn it back is the open question behind the daily swings.
