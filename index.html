Tulsa and Oklahoma

Tulsa City Council votes Wednesday, Nov. 18, on spending $6.6 million of the $18.5 million surplus in the 2016 Vision economic-development sales tax, a temporary tax that runs through 2031 and collected more than projected. The list: $2 million toward parking at The Irving, a roughly $60 million Brookside office-retail project; $1.5 million for a childcare center at Tulsa International Airport; $1.5 million to close the gap on the BOK Eastside incubator at Plaza Santa Cecilia; $1 million for afterschool pilots in TPS and Union; and $750,000 for a Mohawk Park golf-course floodgate. At committee, Councilor Lori Decter Wright said the afterschool money looks headed for a sole-source contract without saying so out loud. The point to watch: when a tax overperforms, the surplus goes to new projects. It is not returned and the rate does not drop. Public comment opens at 5 p.m. Several of these projects also draw George Kaiser Family Foundation money, and so does the outlet that reported them (Tulsa Flyer).

TPS has started carrying out last month’s vote to close 17 school sites and relocate one. Staff will call affected families over the next few weeks. City Lights Village, a 75-home supportive-housing site on 23 acres built with more than $26 million in private money, took its first residents Friday.

Oklahoma City extended its data-center moratorium through the end of 2027 for facilities over 75 MW. Edmond extended its own through June 30, 2027. Tulsa, Norman, Broken Arrow and the Cherokee Nation already have pauses. What drives this is load. The question of who pays for the grid buildout is still open after this week’s PSO rate order.

At the Capitol, the Ethics Commission proposed rules that would require disclosure of AI-generated or “synthetic” campaign media, add review of candidate self-loans over $100,000, and bar candidates from paying themselves for campaign work. The public hearing is Dec. 3 at 10 a.m. The rules then go to lawmakers. The state charter board is taking its rejection of a Jewish charter school to the Tenth Circuit, and the case could reach the U.S. Supreme Court. Richard Glossip pleaded guilty to accessory after the fact in the 1997 Van Treese killing. He is set for sentencing next month and could walk on time served after nearly 30 years. That ends a case in which the state’s own AG had confessed error.

Faith, order, money, power

The Iran standoff has settled into a cold blockade. On Day 258, Windward counted 10 ships through Hormuz on Nov. 12, against about 88 a day before the war, and 8 of the 10 ran dark. Iran’s Guard says two tankers on the US-escorted southern route hit mines and burned Thursday night. That is a claim, not yet verified. CENTCOM says it shot down two Iranian drones headed for Bahrain with no damage, and denies Iran’s claim that it destroyed a US radar there. The UN Security Council met today at Bahrain’s request. Incentives: Washington says it doesn’t need a deal and is escorting enough oil to keep prices tolerable. Tehran holds the strait as its only real leverage and won’t talk until its seven conditions are met. The IMF says Kuwait, Qatar and Iraq are contracting about 16% this year anyway. Neither side has a reason to blink this week.

Economics

Wholesale prices rose 0.3% in October (BLS, consensus 0.4%). The year-over-year rate rose to 6.5%, the highest since November 2022. Goods are up 10.2% on the year and intermediate processed goods 14.6%, which is the pipeline for future consumer prices. Chicago Fed President Goolsbee said inflation is spreading into categories that oil and tariffs don’t explain. Markets fully price a rate hike at the Dec. 8–9 meeting, with small odds of a half point. The 10-year Treasury hit about 5.16%, its highest since October, and the dollar index is near 103.4, a 19-month high. Gold sat flat near $3,878 after Thursday’s drop to a one-year low. Real yields and a strong dollar are beating the safe-haven bid for now. Stocks: S&P 500 down 0.4% to 7,561.67 Friday but up 2.5% for the week. Nasdaq fell 0.9% as Nvidia fell below a $4 trillion market value ahead of Tuesday’s earnings, its first since its CEO left. Brent is about $96.

Bitcoin

Price really moved. Bitcoin fell below $70,000 for the first time since August, touching about $67,770, and about $1.6 billion in leveraged positions were wiped out in 24 hours (CoinGlass via CoinDesk). Spot ETFs have had six straight days of outflows and are now negative for 2026, about $1.56 billion out year to date per SoSoValue, after taking in $21 billion in 2025. A higher dollar and a coming hike are pulling money out of the “hedge” faster than inflation is pushing it in. That’s the opposite of the story Bitcoiners tell, and worth saying plainly.
