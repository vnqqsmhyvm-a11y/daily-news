Tulsa and Oklahoma

Early voting is busy. More than 86,000 Oklahomans voted on the first day alone, which points to stronger turnout than recent midterms (The Oklahoman). KJRH reported hour-long waits at the Tulsa County Election Board; the Chandler Park and O’Brien Park sites had shorter lines. Early voting runs 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Friday and 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. Saturday. Election Day is Tuesday, polls 7 to 7. Your sample ballot is on the OK Voter Portal.

The final pre-election finance reports are in for governor. Mike Mazzei has raised about $16.5 million, and $12.9 million of that is his own loans. Take the loans out and he has raised about $4 million, mostly $3.45 million from individuals and $178,000 from PACs. Cyndi Munson has raised $3.2 million with no loans (KOSU, from Ethics Commission filings). National party money is also flowing in: the Republican Governors Association has put $3 million through an Oklahoma PAC and the Democratic Governors Association $500,000 (The Oklahoman). Mazzei has spent about $15 million so far to Munson’s $2.9 million. The Oklahoman also reports Mazzei secured a commercial loan with his shares in his wealth-management firm, Trinity Strategic Wealth. Who the lender is, and on what terms, is the question a $13 million self-loan should raise.

A Broken Arrow woman, a Philippine citizen with a green card, was charged in federal court with four counts, including voting in the 2022 general election as a non-citizen and lying about citizenship on her registration (USA v. Lopez Rios, N.D. Okla.). One charged case, filed six days before an election. It’s real, and it’s also one case; the base rate matters more than the timing.

The Tulsa City Council passed an ordinance, with an emergency clause so it took effect immediately, on human remains and artifacts tied to Greenwood, the 1921 massacre, and historically Black burial sites. It creates a seven-member panel appointed by the mayor, a majority of them descendants, and the city must explain in writing whenever it goes against the descendants’ consensus. Violations are misdemeanors carrying up to a $1,200 fine and 90 days in jail (Tulsa Local News, from the final text). Worth watching is how it touches future digging and construction near those sites, since violations are criminal. The same agenda carried $65.66 million in existing 2020 and 2024 bond balances, which is money voters already approved, not new debt, and declared 10 acres at 2020 N. Mingo, part of Air Force Plant 3, surplus for sale.

A small tribe has become the test case for prediction markets in Oklahoma. The Alabama-Quassarte Tribal Town signed a deal with Kalshi for a betting platform called PokkeTrades. Two men now claim to lead the tribe, the federal Bureau of Indian Affairs lists both as chief, and the faction that didn’t sign plans to sue. Kalshi says the deal stands (Oklahoma Voice; Journal Record). More than 140 tribes oppose it, because it threatens the gaming compacts they pay the state for (KOSU). Follow the money: a federally regulated exchange routing around state gaming law through a sovereign with a disputed government.

Faith, order, money, power

Two tracks on Iran, running at once. The US has told Gulf and regional allies to prepare for possible renewed strikes on Iran after Tuesday’s election, and to protect energy facilities from retaliation. Officials told AP the action could last several days; Trump hasn’t made a final decision. At the same time, Vice President Vance sent a letter through Qatari mediators to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, proposing direct talks with the nuclear program on the table from the start (Axios). Qatar also delivered a draft seven-day de-escalation plan; Tehran called the ideas unacceptable, wants a four-day version instead, and says it’s drafting a reply to Vance. Its demands are sanctions relief, an end to the port blockade, and reopening Hormuz. The timing tells you the incentive. My read, and it’s speculation: nobody wants a war headline before Tuesday, so both the threat and the offer point to the days just after it.

Economics in plain language

The Fed held rates at 3.75% to 4% on Wednesday, as expected, but two officials, Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack, voted to raise instead (Federal Reserve). Dissents for a hike tell you where the pressure is. The statement says inflation remains elevated. Chair Warsh noted first-half growth was revised up to 2.4% and energy prices are rising again, and said financial conditions have tightened but he won’t call them tight. He also said one 2027 meeting will be strategy-only, with no rate vote, which means one fewer chance to move rates next year.

Thursday’s growth number explains the hawks. Real GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter, up from 2.2%. Consumer spending plus business investment grew 4.9% (BEA). Look under the hood: inventories and computer equipment led investment, and much of the equipment was imported. The quarter’s PCE inflation gauge showed 2.1%, down from 5.0%. That’s a three-month average that sits awkwardly next to $100 oil and the Fed’s own warning, so don’t take it as the all-clear. Nominal GDP grew 5.6%, which is the number debt holders care about.

The AI spending boom took a hit. Alphabet fell about 13%, its worst day ever and roughly $500 billion in value, after revenue missed and search-ad growth slowed to 12%. Free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion. Google Cloud revenue doubled to $30.7 billion, but the company kept this year’s capital spending at $195 billion to $205 billion and said 2027 will be significantly higher (CNBC). When even Google spends more cash than it brings in to build data centers, the question is how long markets keep funding that bet. The Nasdaq fell 1.4%; the S&P 500 lost 0.3% to 7,720.

Oil edged lower on the diplomacy. December WTI settled at $102.00, down 78 cents, after dipping below $100 intraday. Brent settled at $114.88, down 44 cents. Diesel futures fell about 8 cents to $4.74 a gallon (DTN; Reuters matches). The 10-year Treasury yield ended near 5%. Gold sits around $4,030, and bitcoin slid about 3% to near $79,000 with no policy or protocol news behind it.
