North Tulsa TIF heads to Council

The Tulsa Planning Commission backed the tax-increment financing plan for Peak at Parkway, a proposed $200 million mixed-use project on about 40 acres east of Tisdale Parkway between Apache and Pine. The plan authorizes up to $86.2 million across four TIF districts: $28.78 million for public infrastructure and $57.5 million in direct developer financing assistance, each district running up to 25 years. It projects $105.2 million in new tax revenue. That projection is the developer’s and the city’s, not an audited number. Follow the incentive: two-thirds of the TIF money is subsidy to the developer, and the tax increment it captures is revenue the general fund and other taxing bodies don’t see for a generation. The City Council still has to vote; the developers want to break ground by month’s end. (Tulsa Local News, citing the Tulsa Flyer and Oklahoma Eagle. Reporting.)

TPS closures: vote Oct. 19

Tulsa Public Schools has proposed closing 17 schools to cut costs, and the board votes Oct. 19. Families at Sequoyah and Springdale pitched merging both into the Sequoyah building, pointing out that about $15.8 million in 2021 and 2026 bond money is earmarked for repairs at those two schools and, they say, hasn’t been spent. If that holds up, it’s a fair question for every closure on the list: what happens to bond money voters approved for buildings the district now wants to shut? (KJRH. Reporting; the unspent-money claim is the families’.)

Rates still set the weather

The 10-year Treasury closed Friday near 5.26% after touching 5.36% earlier in the week, a 24-year high, and the 30-year sits around 5.6%. Reuters reporting points to a widening gap between 10- and 30-year yields, about 37 basis points, which means investors want extra pay to hold long debt. That’s a term-premium signal about deficits and inflation risk, not about the Fed’s next move. One named contributor: AI hyperscalers issuing heavy long-dated corporate debt, with buyers hedging by selling Treasuries. The AI buildout is now showing up in everyone’s borrowing costs. (BusinessWorld/Reuters. Reporting plus market opinion.)

Oil: Hormuz and a hurricane

Brent stayed above $104 into the weekend. Trump’s no-strike-before-the-midterms line knocked prices down briefly, then Hurricane Isaias shut in more than 60% of U.S. Gulf offshore crude, about 1.3 million barrels a day. Weekend reports describe strikes near Riyadh’s airport, airlines pausing Saudi service, new U.S. sanctions on Iran’s shadow fleet, and early Monday-futures chatter of Brent near $106 after Trump reportedly rejected an Iranian proposal to reopen the strait. Treat the weekend items as unconfirmed until primary sources and Monday’s open show them. Diesel at record highs is the transmission line to grocery and freight prices. Gold sits near $4,190.

Sports

OU stunned No. 1 Texas 27–24 at the Cotton Bowl on Tate Sandell’s 47-yard field goal as time expired; the Sooners are 3-2 (1-1 SEC), and it’s their first win over an AP No. 1 since 2007. No. 18 Oklahoma State beat UCF 42–28 to go 4-1 (2-0 Big 12), its fourth straight win.
