Tulsa and Oklahoma

State Farm will reopen Oklahoma wind and hail roof claims after a state exam found it improperly denied or underpaid them. The Oklahoma Insurance Department’s settlement totals more than $45 million: at least $35 million for homeowners (more if approved claims run past that), $10 million for the Strengthen Oklahoma Homes fortified-roof grant program, and $2.86 million in fines and exam costs paid to the department. If your loss date fell between Jan. 1, 2021 and Dec. 31, 2025 and the claim closed with no payment, you’re eligible. That includes partial payments and claims ruled under the deductible. An independent administrator reviews each claim, but you have to opt in after State Farm mails a notice, and payment isn’t guaranteed. Incoming commissioner Bob Sullivan says he got about five minutes’ notice and calls it “a great deal for State Farm.” Under the deal, the department can’t add penalties for the covered conduct and can’t re-examine the same matters for 12 months after he takes office Jan. 11. Commissioner Glen Mulready leaves office on a headline number. His successor inherits the limits.

The new Legislature is sworn in next week: senators Monday at 2 p.m., House members Tuesday at 10 a.m. The Senate stays 40–8 Republican. Democrats picked up six House seats, making it 75–26, still a supermajority. Kevin Hern plans to take his U.S. Senate oath Nov. 30. Governor-elect Mike Mazzei has proposed ending property taxes for seniors and veterans. So far that’s a proposal with no bill and no price tag. Watch for who covers the lost school and county revenue.

Locally, Hillcrest and Aetna are still out of network with each other after their contract lapsed Nov. 1. Aetna says the two sides “remain far apart,” and about 15,000 Oklahomans are affected. Emergency care is still covered at in-network levels. Tulsa International was still logging cancellations Saturday after Friday’s national ground stop (more below). TU’s homecoming game moved to 6:15 tonight, and OU–Ole Miss moved to Sunday.

Faith, order, money, power

Iran’s navy says seven ships hit mines in or near the Strait of Hormuz in the last 24 hours, with two more tankers burning Saturday in the eastern strait. Iran says the ships had switched off their trackers and taken unauthorized routes. The Guard also says it shot down a US MQ-1 Predator over Qeshm Island. As of this evening I found no independent confirmation of either claim. The latest UK maritime (UKMTO/JMIC) advisory I could find is dated Nov. 12, and CENTCOM hasn’t confirmed anything. Treat these as Tehran’s claims. Incentive: if shippers believe the US-escorted route is mined, the blockade holds without Iran firing a verifiable shot. Washington’s incentive runs the other way, toward saying the lane is clear. In Yemen, the Saudi-led coalition says it downed two Houthi drones aimed at Riyadh and the south.

Security watch

Three items cleared the bar this week.

FAA nationwide ground stop (Fri., Nov. 13). Confirmed by the FAA’s Command Center advisory, an FAA statement, and DOT’s Saturday briefing with Secretary Duffy and Administrator Bedford: all US departures were held from about 2:54 p.m. to 7:15 p.m. CT, and about 3,500 aircraft already airborne were landed safely. DOT’s stated cause: at a remote FAA site, contractor L3Harris (which owns the FAA’s air-traffic network) was moving a line from old copper to fiber. A router then began flooding other routers with more than 1,000 connection attempts every 4–5 seconds, which degraded radio links with aircraft and radar feeds. DOT says it hasn’t determined whether the router was faulty or the install was botched. Homeland Security Secretary Mullin says there’s no evidence of a cyberattack. Reported: about 10,000 cancellations by late Friday (Flightradar24 via Reuters), the longest US shutdown since 9/11, and 3,700+ more Saturday morning (FlightAware). Speculation: anything about sabotage. Nothing official supports it. Incentive: DOT gets to blame both the contractor and years of underinvestment, which strengthens its case for modernization money. Why it matters: the network that runs US airspace still has copper links that can be single points of failure. Duffy expects recovery before Thanksgiving.

Microsoft 365 global outage (Nov. 12–14). Confirmed by Microsoft: Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and Copilot went down after “an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade.” Microsoft says it detected no malicious activity, and its status page showed all products working Saturday. Reported: outage reports from 70+ countries, hitting courts, hospitals, governments, airlines and banks. Still open: Microsoft has promised a full post-incident report. Why it matters for work: emails delayed in the backlog arrive stamped with the delivery time, not the send time, so don’t re-send.

Nvidia and Jensen Huang. Confirmed by Nvidia’s SEC filing (8-K): the board accepted Huang’s resignation as CEO, president and director on Nov. 11, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, longtime director Harvey Jones is chair, and the filing gives no reason. Reported (Reuters, NBC, CNBC, citing sources): the trigger was his relationship with Alice Huang, a businesswoman US intelligence reportedly believes is a Chinese agent. She worked with Singapore customer Megaspeed, which the Justice Department is reported to be investigating for diverting export-restricted AI chips to China. Reuters also reports he misled Nvidia’s general counsel about the relationship, and the board’s independent probe continues. I’ve seen no DOJ filing or charge. Public: two large holders, Altimeter and Atreides, posted a letter asking for a slow CEO search that leaves room for Huang’s return. Speculation: whether any chips actually reached China through this channel, and whether he comes back. Incentive: holders want the founder premium back in the stock, and the board wants distance from an export-control problem. Why it matters: Nvidia sits at the center of US chip-export policy. Earnings come Tuesday under an interim CEO.

Economics

It was a quiet Saturday with markets closed. The week’s setup stands. October wholesale prices were up 6.5% from a year earlier, and markets fully price a Fed hike at the Dec. 8–9 meeting. Nvidia reports Tuesday. Now that its CEO is gone, its web of financing deals with its own customers gets a harder look. The ground stop and the Microsoft outage hit productivity Thursday and Friday, but nobody has published a credible dollar figure, so I won’t guess.
