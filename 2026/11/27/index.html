Water mains give out on a holiday night

A 12-inch line burst at 31st and Memorial just before midnight Thursday. It shut off water to four businesses and closed southbound Memorial between 31st and 32nd for much of Friday (KRMG, FOX23, citing the city’s outage page). Crews were also working a second break near 46th and Sheridan that hit six businesses, and a broken valve near 41st and 129th East Avenue that cut water to 25 homes and four businesses for an estimated 8 to 10 hours (KTUL). That’s three failures in one day. How much of the system is old pipe, and how fast the city replaces it, is a budget question worth asking when the next capital plan comes up.

The Senate leader wants another run at Medicaid expansion

Senate President Pro Tem Lonnie Paxton says he’ll try again in 2027 to send voters a state question that takes Medicaid expansion out of the state constitution and puts it in ordinary law, where legislators could change or repeal it (KGOU/StateImpact). The numbers (as reported): 239,186 Oklahomans are covered under the 2020 expansion. Washington pays 90% and the state pays 10%. The Health Care Authority asked for nearly $500 million more this year and got about half. The 2026 attempt died because the Freedom Caucus and Senate Democrats blocked it. Paxton now says enough Freedom Caucus senators are gone to get it through. The incentive: the state’s 10% share keeps growing, and legislators want control over it without having to go back to voters every time. For now it’s a podcast interview, not a bill.

Nuclear campus: talk, no deals

Five towns, Guymon among them, are pitching to host a federal nuclear campus billed at $50 billion. The Commerce Department says “nothing really formalized” yet and no land deals are being negotiated. DOE is expected to pick three states by year’s end (KOCO). Until DOE picks a state, the $50 billion is a hope, not money anyone has committed.

Another Saudi refinery hit

Confirmed (Saudi Energy Ministry, via Saudi Gazette and Sabq): a “hostile aerial projectile” struck Aramco’s Jazan refinery Thursday, causing a fire that was put out. It killed a Nepalese worker, injured several others, and temporarily halted some operations. A day earlier the Houthis claimed a strike on Ras Tanura, the kingdom’s biggest refinery and export terminal. The incentive: Iran’s allies are raising the cost of Gulf oil for everyone who buys it, and Saudi Arabia is now a target. OPEC+ meets Sunday on 2027 quotas. What the group actually sets will tell you more than what delegates say beforehand.

U.S. jet disables a ship running the Iran blockade

Confirmed (U.S. Central Command): on Nov. 26 a U.S. aircraft fired precision munitions into the stern of the Palau-flagged bulk carrier Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman after it left Bandar Abbas and ignored repeated warnings. Its propulsion was knocked out and the crew was unharmed. CENTCOM says 158 ships have turned back when ordered and six have now been disabled. Reported (AP): ship trackers listed its destination as Singapore. Claim, unverified: Iran’s navy chief says commandos took back two Iranian tankers the U.S. had seized, boarding them “while U.S. personnel were asleep.” He gave no ship names, dates or locations, and the U.S. hasn’t confirmed it (Anadolu, Mehr). Why it matters: the U.S. is now firing on merchant ships to enforce the blockade, so each incident risks escalating. The incentive: Washington wants the blockade to hold so Iran’s oil money dries up, and Tehran wants to look like it can break it, even if only on state TV.

Nvidia: restricted Blackwell chips turn up at the Shanghai site. Reported (Bloomberg, from loan filings submitted to Chinese regulators and lenders): Shanghai Shuoyao pledged a GB300 NVL72 rack, one of Nvidia’s most powerful systems, as collateral for a 96 million yuan ($14 million) loan from Shanghai Innovation Bank. Bloomberg ran the rack’s serial number through a public warranty database, and it traces back to a rack built by Taiwan’s Gigabyte. A second firm listed a GB200 server and 32 H200 servers at the same Yangtze River Delta site, as collateral for a loan from a leasing company owned by a Shanghai government agency. The site is tied to Alice Huang’s Megaspeed network. Shipping any of these chips to China requires a U.S. license. Not established: how the hardware got there. Bloomberg says it has no evidence Megaspeed diverted it, Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, and Gigabyte says it complies with the law. No U.S. agency has commented. Why it matters: these filings are the first paper trail showing banned top-end chips at the site, and they show Chinese lenders with government ties lending against the hardware. It lands days before Jensen Huang’s closed-door House interview Tuesday. The incentive: Chinese buyers pay a premium for banned chips, so middlemen have every reason to route them through countries without restrictions. Bloomberg says that’s the usual path. FAA: still no official root cause. The FAA’s public Q&A is still dated Nov. 15.

Economy and markets

Stocks closed out their best week in more than a year in a half-day session. The Dow rose 1.2% to 50,774.94, the S&P 500 rose 1.6% to 7,297.49, and the Nasdaq rose 2.3%. The S&P gained 5.6% on the week, its best since April 2025, and the Dow had its biggest weekly gain since October 2022 (CNBC, AP). Gold bounced hard: December futures settled at $3,643.80, up $108 (Brownfield). Silver futures jumped about 8% intraday (CNBC). Both are still down for November. Oil eased on the day, with Brent at $95.44 and WTI at $83.08, but Brent is up 3% for the week on the Gulf attacks (CNBC, Xinhua).

Black Friday

Adobe puts U.S. online spending at $12.9 billion, up 9.2% from last year, and says buy-now-pay-later spending is at a record. Those are nominal dollars. With PCE inflation at 3.8%, real growth is closer to 5% (my arithmetic). More of it is going on installment plans, which means credit, not cash, is carrying a bigger share of the holiday.

Sports

OU (7–4) plays at Missouri Saturday at 3:15 p.m. CT, and Oklahoma State (7–4) hosts Kansas at 5:15 to close the regular season.
