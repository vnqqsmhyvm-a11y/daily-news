Tulsa and Oklahoma

Tulsa and Jenks told the public Monday night that the south Tulsa low-water dam is $40 million to $90 million short of what voters approved in 2016. The full version with a bridge and amenities is now about $194 million, and the bare dam about $144 million, against an $80 million estimate a decade ago. Construction can’t start until the gap closes, and the plan is state and federal grants plus private partners. That means the next ask lands on taxpayers further up the chain, so watch for the grant applications and any new local money request, not the renderings. (News On 6 reporting from the meeting.)

The city and Tulsa County signed a five-year deal to book municipal arrests into the David L. Moss jail, replacing the city lockup that closed in July. Contract dollars weren’t in the announcement; check the council agenda for the price. Separately, the city’s defective Neptune ultrasonic water meters are still producing complaints. The city said in October that all of roughly 9,200 recently installed meters will be replaced, and that affected accounts are on estimated billing. One family reports the same $215 bill for eight months, and nobody has said yet whether overbilled customers get refunds.

PSO broke ground Monday on two gas turbines, about 450 megawatts, at its Oologah plant. They’re due online by the end of 2028. That’s real steel for the large-load buildout, and it will go into the rate base. Last week’s 3.55% residential rate increase is the reminder of who pays.

The Oklahoma Highway Patrol’s report on Monday’s Turner Turnpike crash says a Tulsa Public Schools vehicle carrying Webster High students hit the back corner of a semi that was pulling onto the turnpike from the mile 170 rest stop. One 16-year-old was killed. As of Tuesday one student was still in the hospital.

The State Election Board scheduled certification of the Nov. 3 state and federal results for today. When checked this evening, its results page still said “unofficial.” The unofficial totals have Mazzei at 582,039 and Munson at 514,776. Republicans hold the House 75–26, one seat short of the three-fourths needed to raise taxes without a vote of the people, and the Senate 40–8. In the 1st Congressional District, Tedford won 52.3% to 47.7%.

Washington and the war

The House voted 211–204 to send Rep. Thomas Massie’s impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, which kills them. Massie was the only Republican voting no. The articles charged an undeclared war with Iran, the Caribbean boat strikes, and the Maduro seizure. The vote is the deed here. A Republican House that has complained about the war for months would not put its name on constitutional war-powers charges. Massie says he won’t refile, and Democrats take both chambers in January, so this probably isn’t over.

On Iran, words and deeds still point in different directions. Trump called the war “militarily effectively over” on Monday, and Hegseth claims more than 13,000 targets struck and US control of Hormuz. Iran’s security chief Mohsen Rezaei says no talks are underway and the strait stays closed until his country’s seven demands are met. Tuesday, the Revolutionary Guards claimed three tankers burning in the strait, ADNOC said one of its ships was attacked in UAE waters, and the UK’s maritime agency (UKMTO) reported a tanker hit by a projectile off Oman. The International Maritime Organization counts 130 incidents and at least 25 seafarers killed since February, and October was the worst month for ship attacks yet. The US blockade of Iranian ports continues. Whose claims hold up shows in the shipping counts, not the press conferences.

Money

October consumer prices rose 0.4%, and core prices (without food and energy) rose 0.3%. Both beat forecasts, and it’s the third straight month of 0.4% or more. There’s no official yearly figure, because last fall’s shutdown wiped out the October 2025 data. The Cleveland Fed estimates 3.8% a year, and economists’ estimates run 3.9% to 4.0%. Food rose 0.5%, with beef up 0.9%, and shelter made up over 30% of the increase. Markets already price a December Fed hike at around 80%. Oil is falling, but the drop isn’t reaching the grocery aisle yet.

Monday’s rally was about oil. The S&P 500 jumped 2.35% to 7,549.94, its best day since April, and Brent fell 5.85% to $92.57. The 2-year Treasury yield still rose to 4.56%. The bond market is betting the Fed raises rates even if the war cools. Gold closed at $4,114.50.

Bitcoin

Bitcoin is near $73,600 and didn’t join the stock rally. US spot ETFs lost another $201 million Monday, per Farside, and about $1.5 billion since Nov. 2. They’re now about $1.1 billion negative for the year, after more than $20 billion of inflows in each of the past two years. The miner MARA disclosed it sold 13,597 BTC (about $1.1 billion) over six weeks and plans to sell more. Big holders are selling, which undercuts the safe-haven story for now.

Sports

OU hosts Ole Miss Saturday at 3:15. Venables says Jason Witten could keep the offensive coordinator job if the offense stays consistent, and WR Trell Harris likely won’t play again this season after a post-surgery infection. At the GM meetings, the Cardinals and JoJo Romero have mutual interest in a reunion, and the Braves are shopping for starting pitching and say they won’t move Acuña.
