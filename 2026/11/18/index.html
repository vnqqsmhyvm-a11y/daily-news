Peak on the Parkway TIF goes to its first hearing tonight

The 5 p.m. council agenda has the first public hearing on the North Tulsa project plan: new tax-increment districts that capture property tax, and in some cases sales tax too, with the Tulsa Authority for Economic Opportunity running the money. The second hearing is Dec. 2. Two other items on tonight’s agenda are worth watching (City of Tulsa agenda via Oklahoma Record). One is a proposed extension of the city’s data-center building-permit moratorium through March 2027. The other is a three-year police drone pilot paid for by a private foundation donation. Broken Arrow pushed its own data-center pause out to mid-2027 this week, so two of the metro’s biggest cities are now holding off large loads until zoning catches up. The drone pilot is a surveillance tool funded by private money. Ask who sets the retention and use rules, and whether the council writes them or the donor’s grant terms do. Results weren’t posted at press time.

2027 bills start dropping

Filing opened Wednesday (KOCO). The first three bills: SB 1 would let medical-marijuana cardholders get a handgun license unless something else disqualifies them. SB 2 would ban certain additives in food served at public schools and state facilities. HB 1001 would restrict government surveillance technology and kill the camera-based uninsured-vehicle program. Filing a bill isn’t passing one, so track these by committee votes. The Legislature is now 40–8 in the Senate and 75–26 in the House. Pro Tem Lonnie Paxton was renominated without opposition. The Freedom Caucus lost its vice chair in a primary, and its chair, Sen. Shane Jett, is term-limited (KOSU/KGOU).

Tribes vs. the Senate permitting bill

The Senate voted 70–27 Monday to end debate on S. 5653, the “Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act,” and a final vote is expected before Thanksgiving. Both Oklahoma senators voted for cloture. The Muscogee and Cherokee Nations oppose it because it narrows tribal Section 106 consultation on historic and sacred sites (KOSU). The trade-off is real. Faster permitting for power plants, pipelines and mines is the abundant-energy case. The cost is less leverage for tribes, which hold real treaty power in this state. Read the final text before believing either side’s summary.

Also: Gov.-elect Mazzei added business, ag, energy and finance names to his transition team, including the Choctaw chief (Tulsa World, paywalled, headline only). The state confirmed its first pediatric flu death of the season, and Tulsa County has 40 flu hospitalizations (News On 6).

Iran widens the exchange to Kuwait

Iran’s army says it sent drones at Ali Al Salem Air Base, which hosts U.S. forces, after U.S. jets hit a missile site on Qeshm Island. It’s the first attack on Kuwait in over two months. Kuwait says its air defenses engaged the drones and reported no damage. Bahrain says it intercepted more attacks, its fourth in six days. ADNOC says a crew member is missing after one of its tankers was hit near Hormuz Tuesday (BBC, AP). Iran’s armed forces chief threatened a response “without geographic limitations.” Here’s the action-versus-words check: crude still fell Wednesday, so traders are betting on surplus, not on a closed strait. Qatar, which is mediating, called the attacks a “dangerous escalation.”

Shenzhen trilateral

China’s foreign ministry confirms Xi will host Trump and Putin at the APEC meetings overnight. It’s the first three-way summit. No joint communiqué is expected. Judge it by what gets signed (trade terms, export rules, Iran, Ukraine), not by the photo.

NSA network: back up, and now with a named cause

Confirmed (NSA statement, 11/17): “temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance.” On the record (DNI Jay Clayton, Fox Business): systems failed to come back up after patching for a PHP web-software vulnerability, and “that is what has happened at the NSA.” Reported (CBS, two sources): NSA Net is back up, but staggered arrivals (“Code Gray”) continue because the system may not handle everyone logging in at once. CBS also reports that the flaw, dubbed PHP2Shell, prompted an urgent FBI/NSA/CISA patch-now advisory on Oct. 21. I haven’t opened that advisory myself. Speculation: CBS says the patching rush “may help explain” other recent outages. Nobody has shown that, and the FAA failure was hardware. Why it matters: the official story moved from “routine maintenance” to “an emergency security patch broke it.” That’s more honest and less flattering. The incentive behind the first statement was to make it sound smaller.

FAA ground stop: failure reproduced, cause still unknown, blame shifting. Confirmed (DOT, Sec. Duffy): the El Dorado, Ark., failure has been recreated at the L3Harris lab, with L3Harris, HPE/Juniper, Cisco, FAA and FBI on site. It was the 69th install of that router, and the first 68 worked. The root cause is still unknown. Confirmed (HPE): it believes its gear “was working as configured.” Reported (AP): Duffy said Wednesday the stop might have been shorter if L3Harris had caught the problem sooner. Why it matters: the vendor says the configuration was fine, DOT is pointing at the contractor, and the Post’s earlier report put an FAA sign-off in the chain. Everyone’s incentive is to be the one who wasn’t holding it, and that fight decides who pays and whether the $50B ask gets strings attached.

Nvidia / Megaspeed: Congress asks for briefings

Confirmed (House Energy and Commerce): Chairman Guthrie wrote to Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress and to Commerce’s Lutnick and Kessler, asking for briefings by Dec. 2. Reported (NBC): Nvidia’s board formed a committee on national security and export-control compliance. Fifteen national-security and AI-policy figures asked AG Blanche to investigate whether Nvidia kept selling after it had evidence of diversion. A source says Nvidia itself is not a target. DOJ won’t comment. No charges have been filed.

Microsoft 365: nothing new; the final post-incident report is due Friday.

Economy and markets

Fed minutes: hikes coming, and AI debt is on their worry list. The October minutes (Fed release) say “almost all participants” expect further hikes to “likely be appropriate.” The vote to hold at 3.75–4.00% was 10–2, with Cook and Hammack dissenting for a hike. Staff flagged that “off-balance-sheet leasing commitments and circular financing arrangements among AI-related firms could amplify losses if returns on AI investment were to disappoint.” Most participants said AI-related debt grew fast through “complex and opaque financing structures.” Governor Waller told CNBC the Fed may need to “shock people” with a half-point in December. Markets price a quarter point on Dec. 9 (CNBC). October CPI ran +0.4% headline and +0.3% core. Plain version: the central bank now says out loud that the AI boom is being financed in ways it can’t fully see. That’s the credit-cycle warning to watch.

Anthropic prices the biggest IPO ever, $81B at $83 a share (company release; about 980M shares; trades Thursday as ANT). It lands while the S&P is on a five-day slide and Nvidia is down about 26% this month (CNBC). The public is being offered AI equity right as the Fed flags AI leverage. Watch how it trades, not the first-day headline.
