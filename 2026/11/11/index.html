Tulsa and Oklahoma

Mayor Monroe Nichols gave his second State of the City Tuesday night. What he claimed: city revenue is outpacing spending for the first time since 2022; $100 million went into roads and infrastructure; a five-year, $500 million sewer and stormwater repair program has started; homicides are down 17% and property crime is down by double digits; 820 people were housed through Safe Move Tulsa and 25 camps were closed; and 4,400 housing units have been finished or permitted since December 2024, about 2,500 of them priced as affordable, toward a goal of 6,000 by 2028. All of that is the mayor’s own scorecard (speech text on cityoftulsa.org). The budget claim is the one to check against the city’s audited financials before anyone repeats it. The speech didn’t cover last month’s 4–4 council vote that killed his public-safety oversight committee (KJRH).

Housing note: the Tulsa Housing Authority tied 20 federal HUD-VASH vouchers to Golf Course Studios, a 59-unit former nursing home near Mohawk Park, for homeless veterans (FOX23). These are project-based vouchers, so the federal rent money stays with those units and doesn’t follow the tenant.

At the Capitol, Senate Republicans re-nominated Lonnie Paxton as president pro tem behind closed doors. He ran unopposed, and the full Senate votes Jan. 5. Oklahoma Voice reports his moderate bloc should grow because some Freedom Caucus senators lost. Governor-elect Mike Mazzei is again pitching property-tax relief for seniors and veterans (KJRH). Keep in mind that a week ago voters rejected SQ 847’s smaller assessment caps, 54.3% to 45.7%. Any property-tax cut now has to go through the Legislature, where Republicans fell below the three-fourths House majority needed to raise revenue without a public vote. Mazzei says cabinet picks come by the end of November. State and federal results were set for certification Tuesday, and I didn’t find a contest or recount notice.

Faith, order, money, power

The Iran war is in its ninth month, and the US and Iran are telling opposite stories. Rubio, at the G7 in Paris: “The economic campaign is so devastating that right now there is no need for major combat operations.” Iran’s security chief Mohsen Rezaei called talk of talks in Qatar “a pure lie” and said Hormuz stays shut until Washington answers Tehran’s seven conditions, which include lifting sanctions and the blockade and ending the fighting in Lebanon (Al Jazeera/Reuters). Each side has a reason to say what it’s saying. Washington wants oil prices lower. Tehran wants sanctions relief without looking like it gave in. On the water, the IRGC claimed three tankers hit mines Tuesday on an “unauthorised route,” and UKMTO logged two tanker incidents, one hit by a projectile off Oman. October had at least 37 attacks on ships, the most in any month of the war. Calling the war “effectively over” doesn’t match what’s happening in the strait.

Economics and geopolitics

Fed-funds futures now price about a 90% chance of a rate hike at the Dec. 8–9 meeting (CME FedWatch via CoinDesk), after Tuesday’s 0.4% October CPI. In plain terms: prices are rising faster than the Fed’s tools are slowing them, and borrowers are about to pay for it. That includes mortgages and credit cards. Stocks rallied anyway on AI money. CoreWeave rose about 25% on a beat-and-raise quarter, and the S&P 500 closed up 1.31% at 7,619 and the Nasdaq up 1.60% (Motley Fool, Kiplinger). Brent settled up 1.39% at $92.76 (Dow Jones Market Data) but is still down 21% for November. Bond markets were closed for Veterans Day.

Bitcoin

Flat near $74,000 while stocks rose. US spot ETFs saw a fourth straight day of outflows: $95 million on Tuesday, $78 million of it from BlackRock’s IBIT, its eighth straight day of redemptions (SoSoValue via CoinDesk). Options traders are buying downside protection. If bitcoin is supposed to be a hedge against a hiking Fed, the money isn’t acting like it this week.
