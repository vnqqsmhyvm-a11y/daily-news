Council votes tomorrow on public-safety oversight

Wednesday’s regular City Council agenda (filed Oct. 9) keeps Title 16—Independent Review and Oversight of Public Safety—on second reading. Sponsors are Councilors Hall-Harper and Decter Wright. The ordinance would create an Office of Independent Review and Oversight plus a civilian board covering police, fire, animal control, and code enforcement. Also on first reading: a $6.044 million transfer inside TMUA sewer capital (Spunky Creek East Branch Interceptor) and $1.5 million from Vision fund balance for Municipal Courts and Lockup. Second reading also includes a 10-year CoxCom cable franchise (cleanup of a prior scrivener’s error). A resolution urging the Air Force to base the next Air National Guard F-35A squadron with Tulsa’s 138th Fighter Wing is on the agenda—words unless the Pentagon picks Tulsa. The North Tulsa Peak at Parkway TIF (~$86.2 million authorized across four districts after Planning Commission conformance Oct. 7) is still not on Wednesday’s docket. (City of Tulsa Oct. 14 agenda, primary.)

Election clock: 21 days; absentee requests due Oct. 19

The Nov. 3 general is three weeks out. Registration closed Oct. 9. Absentee ballot requests are due Oct. 19. Early voting in Tulsa County runs Oct. 28–30 (8 a.m.–6 p.m.) and Oct. 31 (8 a.m.–2 p.m.). Sample ballot: OK Voter Portal. On the same ballot, State Question 845 would rewrite the Judicial Nominating Commission’s party-balance and attorney rules—who funds the yes/no campaigns matters more than the slogans. Separately, Court of Criminal Appeals Judge Robert L. Hudson is the only judge on that court up for retention this cycle (Judge Gary Lumpkin is retiring; Stitt has until Nov. 2 to name a replacement or the appointment falls to Chief Justice Rowe). Hudson, appointed by Fallin in 2015 and retained in 2016 and 2020, answered a NonDoc questionnaire: originalist framing, jury-sentence holding in Metoyer, and no active opposition campaign visible. A Court of Criminal Appeals judge has never lost retention; voters declined a Supreme Court justice’s retention for the first time in 2024. (Tulsa County / state election calendar; NonDoc on Hudson—process reporting.)

Marijuana testing interim study—proposals, not votes

House members heard an Oct. 13 interim study on medical-marijuana lab testing after a survey found about 30% of patients distrust Oklahoma lab results. OMMA’s quality-assurance lab is operating but still awaiting accreditation for some tests. Rep. T.J. Marti (R–Broken Arrow) urged a pause so recent statutes can bite before more rules; Rep. Jacob Rosecrants (D–Norman) pushed pesticide/hazard testing and prep for federal rescheduling. Treat as process talk until a bill gets a committee vote. (Oklahoma Voice. Reporting.)

Existing-home sales ticked up; rates still the choke

NAR’s September existing-home sales rose 1.5% month-over-month to a 3.99 million seasonally adjusted annual rate—still 2.2% below a year earlier, and NAR’s economist called August–September the two slowest months of 2026. Unsold inventory rose 1.9% to 1.60 million homes (4.8 months’ supply). Median price $417,500, up 1.3% year-over-year (39th straight annual gain). South and West sales rose 2.8% monthly; Northeast fell 2.1%. FRED’s Freddie Mac 30-year fixed for the week ending Oct. 8 is 7.40% (was 7.28% Oct. 1)—that is the credit channel into this print, not a Midtown listing story. Cash buyers were 27% of sales; first-timers 31%. (NAR via HousingWire wrap; FRED/Freddie Mac mortgage series. Reporting / primary.)
