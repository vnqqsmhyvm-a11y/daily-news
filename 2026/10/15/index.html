Public-safety oversight failed on a 4–4 tie

On Wednesday the Council rejected Title 16 (Office of Independent Review and Oversight of Public Safety plus an 11-member civilian board), sponsored by Hall-Harper and Decter Wright. Yes: Hall-Harper, Decter Wright, Dutton, Bellis. No: Bush, Lakin, Bengel, Gilbert. Archie abstained, so it died. The office would have reviewed serious cases (shootings, custody deaths, force causing death or serious injury) across police, fire, emergency management, animal services, code, and city security — advisory only, no discipline power, stated cost about $500,000 a year. Chief Brooks and the FOP opposed; backers pointed to multimillion-dollar settlements and Flock camera questions. A decade of attempts still has not produced this office. (News On 6 vote tally; City of Tulsa agenda, primary. Fact / reporting.)

Tulsa Ports green-lights a suit against Inola’s smelter brakes. Thursday the Ports board voted unanimously to authorize litigation against the Town of Inola over industrial-development restrictions, including the permit moratorium on the proposed aluminum smelter, and retained GableGotwals. Counsel may seek declaratory and injunctive relief; as of late afternoon no filing had been confirmed. Ports says it is a non-appropriated state agency and does not use public ops money for legal costs. Inola officials said they had not heard of a suit. Watch who actually pays for power and water, and whether a court overrides a local moratorium — not the jobs pitch. (KTUL. Reporting; deed is the authorization, not a filed case yet.)

Nov. 3 is 19 days out; absentee requests due Monday

Tulsa County: request an absentee ballot by Oct. 19 (OK Voter Portal or paper to the Election Board). Early voting Oct. 28–30, 8 a.m.–6 p.m., and Oct. 31, 8 a.m.–2 p.m., at the Election Board (12000 E. Skelly), Tulsa Tech Broken Arrow, Chandler Park, and O’Brien Park. On the ballot statewide: SQ 847 (property-tax assessment-growth caps — see Wednesday’s brief), SQ 845 (Judicial Nominating Commission rewrite), and retention of four Supreme Court justices — Darby, Jett, Kane, and Kuehn — with no organized “no” campaign this cycle (unlike 2024, when one justice lost for the first time). Sample ballot from the OK Voter Portal is the source of truth. (Tulsa County Election Board, primary; NonDoc on retention. Fact / reporting.)

Hormuz attacks hit a wartime high; oil is still moving

Maritime trackers count about 26 vessel attacks or security incidents in the first half of October — the highest monthly pace since the war began — with two cargo ships sunk in recent days and another tanker reported afire after entering Iran’s declared prohibited zone with tracking off. Flows through the strait have held in the low-teens of millions of barrels a day on tanker-tracker averages even as Iran widens targets beyond shuttle tankers. CENTCOM still claims large escorted volumes; Tehran’s stated incentive includes pressure ahead of the U.S. midterms — treat that as narrative, not proof. The barrel counts and insurance risk are the deeds. (Lloyd’s List, TankerTrackers via wire wraps, CENTCOM/IRGC claims. Reporting.)

Economics and geopolitics

Wholesale inflation screamed: PPI +1.3% in September, +6.3% year over year. BLS: largest monthly rise since March 2022 and largest 12-month since December 2022. Final-demand goods jumped 2.6% (largest since the series began in 2009); energy alone +10.8%, with gasoline +20.3% accounting for about half the goods rise. Services +0.8%. Final demand less foods, energy, and trade services +0.5% month / +5.0% year — so it is not only fuel. Diesel and trucking are already in the pipeline for retail. Separately, Census retail sales rose 0.8% in September (gas stations +6.4%); ex-autos and gas the gain was only about 0.2%. Energy is inflating the headline and squeezing real volumes elsewhere. (BLS PPI, primary; Census via market wraps. Fact.)

Markets priced less October hike, not more

After the print, CME FedWatch odds of a hike at the Oct. 27–28 meeting fell back toward about 30% (from roughly half after Wednesday’s CPI), as Daly and Bowman sounded deliberate rather than urgent and AI-debt stress pulled money into Treasuries. The 10-year yield settled near 5.09% (about −10 bp on the day); the 2-year near 4.76%. Dow +1.1% to about 51,178; S&P +0.3% to about 7,745; Nasdaq roughly flat as chips and AI names lagged and equal-weight outperformed. WTI settled near $97–98 after EIA counted an ~8.6 million-barrel crude draw (to about 416 million); Brent near $105.92. December gold +1.5% to $4,170. Mortgage rates: Freddie Mac 30-year still 7.40% for the week of Oct. 15. Chair Warsh speaks at the IMF in Bangkok overnight — last Fed voice before the blackout. (BLS/EIA primaries; Newsquawk and Edge settlements; FRED MORTGAGE30US. Fact / reporting.)
