Council votes Wednesday on public-safety oversight

The Oct. 14 City Council agenda puts on second reading an ordinance creating an Office of Independent Review and Oversight of Public Safety—covering police, fire, animal control, and code enforcement—plus a civilian board. Sponsors are Councilors Hall-Harper and Decter Wright. News On 6 has put the initial budget near $500,000 a year. Department chiefs told a Sept. 30 committee the plan duplicates existing oversight; the Fraternal Order of Police opposes it. Follow the incentive: multimillion-dollar police settlements push some councilors toward a paid monitor, while others call a board without subpoena power symbolic. Also on first reading: a $6.044 million transfer inside TMUA sewer capital (Spunky Creek East Branch Interceptor) and $1.5 million from Vision fund balance for Municipal Courts and Lockup. The North Tulsa Peak at Parkway TIF (~$86.2 million authorized, still needing Council approval after Planning Commission) is not on Wednesday’s agenda. (City of Tulsa Oct. 14 agenda, primary. Reporting on budget/opposition from News On 6 / KJRH via local wrap.)

Election clock: absentee requests due Oct. 19

The Nov. 3 general is 22 days out. Registration closed Oct. 9. Absentee ballot requests are due Oct. 19. Early voting in Tulsa County runs Oct. 28–30 (8 a.m.–6 p.m.) and Oct. 31 (8 a.m.–2 p.m.) at four sites: Election Board (12000 E. Skelly), Tulsa Tech Broken Arrow, O’Brien Park, and Chandler Park. Polls Election Day are 7 a.m.–7 p.m. Sample ballot: OK Voter Portal. On Friday’s registration deadline, the state Online Voter Registration System lost contact with Service Oklahoma for a couple of hours; Election Board Secretary Paul Ziriax called it isolated and said nearly 800 applications (~40% of that day’s total) went through after the fix, with a 2026 single-day record of 1,906 electronic submissions. No legal authority to extend the deadline. (Tulsa County Election Board / oklahoma.gov calendar, primary. Oklahoma Voice on the outage—process reporting.)

SQ 845: who picks judges’ shortlists

Nov. 3 also includes State Question 845, a constitutional change to the 15-member Judicial Nominating Commission that sends three names to the governor for many judicial vacancies. Current rules cap how many of the governor’s six appointees can come from one party, reserve six seats for Oklahoma Bar Association picks, and bar certain attorney/family conflicts. The measure would drop party-affiliation caps and some attorney/family bans, realign districts, and allow longer total service (up to 12 years). Senate author Todd Gollihare (R-Sapulpa) argued the present structure underweights the GOP majority; critics say it opens the door to one-party capture of the vetting panel. The Commission dates to a 1967 reform after a bribery scandal. Deeds to watch: who funds the yes/no campaigns, not the slogans. (Oklahoma Voice; ballot title on oklahoma.gov. Reporting / ballot text.)

Ethics Commission opens the file on Rep. Danny Williams

On Oct. 8 the Oklahoma Ethics Commission unanimously released Case 2026-19 materials on Rep. Danny Williams (R–House District 28, Seminole), finding public release in the public interest. The notice alleges substantial evidence of Rules 4.4 (misuse of office), 4.5 (misuse of authority), and 4.7 (conflicts)—including using office to fundraise for and benefit from affiliated nonprofits (Oklahoma Education Commission, Inc.; House of Hope), steering state mental-health grant money, and promoting for-profit health ventures in which he or a relative held interests. The Commission also says he has not been forthright in the probe. These are allegations in an ongoing investigation, not findings of a court. He has (or had) 20 days from the notice to respond; next steps can be settlement or a civil suit. Separate from any House inquiry. (Oklahoma Ethics Commission public notice PDF, primary.)

Charter board again rejects Jewish school after federal win

The Statewide Charter School Board voted 4–3 Monday against approving Ben Gamla Jewish Charter School—one short of the five votes needed—despite a recent Oklahoma City federal court ruling that denying a charter solely for religious character violates the First Amendment. Board member William Pearson cited local Jewish leaders’ opposition and thin enrollment expectations (~10 students year one, per the founder). Chair Brian Shellem voted yes and said the denial “subjects our board to more litigation.” Founders may appeal or seek contempt; attorney A.J. Ferate floated that path. Action over narrative: a federal injunction-style ruling did not produce a board approval. (Oklahoma Voice. Reporting.)

Oil eased on supply talk; gold held the $4,200 area

Brent traded roughly $103–$105 Monday after a brief spike on reports of a Houthi strike near Riyadh’s airport, then faded on talk of more Russian diesel reaching markets and thin holiday volumes. Treat “energy ceasefire” and import-permission headlines as words until barrels and official notices show up. Gold poked above $4,200 early, then settled around that area. Bitcoin spot about $82,500 late afternoon CT—no fresh U.S. policy or protocol trigger today; the signal remains high real yields and a firm dollar. Diesel at the pump is still the transmission line into freight and groceries. (Market wraps; Coinbase spot. Reporting / market print.)
