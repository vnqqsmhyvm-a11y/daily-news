The Lead

Tulsa and Oklahoma

Data centers: who checks the developer’s math? Tulsa County Commissioner Stan Sallee told lawmakers at an interim study he wants a quasi-governmental technical resource that local boards can consult on data-center claims about water, power, noise and setbacks. He says it’s “not more government regulation.” That’s words, not action: no bill and no budget yet. The real question is who pays for the grid and water infrastructure, and that’s still unanswered. Tulsa’s water director was on News On 6’s Hot Seat today on drought and data-center demand. (FOX23, News On 6. That’s reporting; the proposal itself is opinion.)

Debtors' arrests

The state is investigating reports that people are being arrested over unpaid court fees and fines (The Oklahoman, Keaton Ross; paywalled, headline only, so unverified). If it holds up, this is state power hitting the poor for the sake of revenue. Worth watching for which agency is doing the investigating and which counties are involved.

Money and the world

The bond market is the story

The 10-year Treasury briefly topped 5.35% this week, its highest since 2002, and closed Friday around 5.24%. The 30-year sits at 5.63% and the 2-year at 4.80%. Thursday’s $22 billion 30-year auction drew solid demand, and that steadied things for now. Even so, financing $119 billion of debt in one week at these rates is the fiscal bill coming due. Fed minutes showed a split committee under Warsh, and the market is leaning toward a hike rather than a cut.

Oil and Iran

Brent ran above $105 on Hormuz worries, then eased to about $103.8 (WTI around $91) after Trump said there’d be no strike on Iran before the Nov. 3 midterms. Notice the incentive: the timing of a strike is being set by the election calendar. Gold finished near $4,195 to $4,219, its first weekly gain in three weeks. The dollar and gold rising together is a haven signal, not a debasement signal.

AI froth check

OpenAI’s annualized revenue run-rate was reported restated to about $50 billion, against the roughly $68 billion figure circulating in September. The Nasdaq fell 1.25% that day. When the hype numbers get revised down, so does the valuation. (Market-wrap reporting; not checked against a primary source.)

Bitcoin: nothing moved on policy or protocol. Skip.

Sports

No. 18 Oklahoma State beat UCF 42–28 in Stillwater to go 4-1 (2-0 Big 12); Mestemaker threw for 304 yards. OU lost the Red River game to No. 1 Texas, 20–17.
