Oversight vote tonight; result not in yet

The Council took up Title 16 (Office of Independent Review and Oversight of Public Safety plus a civilian board, sponsors Hall-Harper and Decter Wright) at its 5 p.m. meeting. Today’s Urban and Economic Development committee agenda had the Public Safety Commissioner and the Terence Crutcher Foundation scheduled on the same item. Five votes pass it. Stated budget is about $500,000 a year; the office would be advisory only, with no subpoena power and no authority to discipline. Backers point to repeated multimillion-dollar police settlements that produced no policy changes; Gilbert, Archie, and Bengel have voiced doubts, and the mayor’s office had not taken a position as of late September. No outcome had been reported when this was published. (City of Tulsa agenda, primary; Tulsa Dispatch and News On 6 on positions. Reporting.)

AG moves to join the Sand Springs data-center fight

On Monday, Attorney General Drummond filed a 17-page motion to intervene in Tulsa County case CV-2026-509. In that case, Protect Sand Springs Alliance challenges the city’s June 2025 annexation of about 826 acres in Osage County for Project Spring, a Google hyperscale campus. The city reached the site through a strip about 100 feet wide and nearly seven miles long with no city services in it. Sand Springs argues neighbors lack standing; if the state gets in, that defense mostly goes away. A second suit (CV-2026-439, Land Legacy) attacks the rezoning over conservation deed restrictions. The city’s own pitch is more than $100 million over about 20 years. No hearing has been held and no ruling has been made. Watch who is actually paying for the substations and the water, not the revenue claim. (News 9, Keystone Gusher, KRMG. Reporting; filing not yet read.)

Stitt pauses state dyed-diesel enforcement for 120 days

Monday’s letter orders the Tax Commission, DPS, and the Corporation Commission to suspend state taxes, fines, and enforcement on red-dyed diesel in farm vehicles on highways, and tells the Tax Commission to ask the IRS for penalty relief. The state part is real action; the federal tax and penalty still stand until the IRS acts. Reported savings are about 20 cents a gallon. (Governor’s order via House Agriculture chairs’ release; reporting.)

SQ 847 on the Nov. 3 ballot: a property-tax cap

Legislative Referendum 381 would cut the annual cap on assessed-value growth from 3% to 1.75% for homesteads and farmland and from 5% to 4% for other real property starting in tax year 2027, and convert the senior freeze into income-based caps. A University of Central Oklahoma professor’s estimate, cited by OK Policy (an advocacy think tank), puts the lost county and school revenue at $42 million in year one, rising to $575 million by year 10. Treat that as an advocate’s high-end figure, not settled fact. The real question is whether levies and bond millages get raised to make up the difference. Election is 20 days out; absentee requests are due Monday, Oct. 19. (State Election Board, primary; ballot detail via Deer Creek Schools and OK Policy. Reporting/advocacy.)

Flock ruling, two weeks on

U.S. District Judge Sara Hill (N.D. Okla.) held Oct. 1 in U.S. v. Kyle that a Tulsa County deputy’s warrantless pull of about 30 days of a car’s license-plate camera history across several states violated the Fourth Amendment, and she suppressed the evidence. She called the system “indiscriminate mass surveillance.” It binds no other court and an appeal is expected, but any Tulsa-area agency running Flock queries without a warrant now has a live suppression risk. (Court order, primary.)

National and the world

Iran is squeezing Hormuz again, and says the target is our midterms. Kpler counts 10 tankers struck Sept. 28–Oct. 4. UKMTO logged 15 more attacks Oct. 6–14, 11 of them in the last four days, and one cargo ship sank after hitting a mine. Oil through the strait fell to 2.9 million barrels on Oct. 11, down from 20.8 million on Oct. 8 (the prewar average was 17.1 million a day). CENTCOM says it helped move more than 19 million barrels on Monday. Separately, a Houthi strike hit a building at Aramco’s Dhahran headquarters, with no casualties reported. An Iranian official told Reuters the change in tactics is partly meant to sway the November U.S. vote. Treat that as Tehran’s stated incentive, not proof it works. The barrel counts are the part that matters. (Al Jazeera citing Kpler, UKMTO, and CENTCOM; AP. Reporting.)

CPI ran hot: 3.7% a year, 0.6% a month

BLS says September CPI rose 0.6% (the biggest monthly gain in five months) and 3.7% over the year, up from 3.4% in August. Core rose 0.3% for the month and 2.6% for the year, both a tick above forecasts. Gasoline drove more than half the monthly jump. The core number is the warning, because it means price pressure is spreading beyond fuel. Fed funds futures now put about a 37% chance on another hike at the Oct. 27–28 meeting, up from 22% Tuesday; the Fed already raised rates in September. Goolsbee called the print “discouraging.” (BLS via CNBC and Reuters; CME FedWatch via Reuters. Primary data / reporting.)

The Fed’s own regional survey describes a squeeze close to home. The Kansas City Fed’s section of today’s Beige Book (that district covers Oklahoma) says diesel and shipping costs are squeezing margins up and down supply chains. Shoppers are buying cheaper and eating at home. Small businesses are leaning on high-cost merchant cash advances as SBA lending tightens. CRE lenders report “spreads rose substantially,” and data-center construction is crowding out other manufacturing and labor. Oil and gas drilling rose moderately, and some contacts in rural Colorado reported spot diesel shortages. Bankers expect farm and consumer loans to weaken. These are anecdotes from the Fed’s business contacts, not measured data, but they line up with the CPI print. (Federal Reserve Beige Book, Tenth District, primary.)

Markets: stocks had their worst day since July; bonds held up better. The S&P 500 fell about 1.1% and the Nasdaq 1.9%. Tech fell on AI-spending worries, with Oracle credit costs and CoreWeave bond yields near 15% in focus. The 10-year spiked to 5.32% on the print, then closed near 5.19%. The 2-year touched 4.95% and finished about 4.81%. Bond traders are pricing over-tightening risk more than a runaway Fed. Daily mortgage-rate trackers had the 30-year fixed at about 7.47%. WTI settled at $95.09 (a three-week high) and Brent at $106.06. December gold fell 1.66% to $4,107.90 and silver dropped about 6% as the dollar rallied. Bitcoin slid about 1.6% to roughly $81,800, trading with risk assets today, not as a hedge. Powell speaks late Thursday. (Market wraps: investingLive, The Edge commodities wrap, Cboe; CoinDesk and Tindex for BTC. Reporting.)
