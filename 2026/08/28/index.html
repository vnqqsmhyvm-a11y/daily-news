Friday, August 28, 2026

Tulsa and Oklahoma

Late this morning a repo fight at Mike’s Towing on North Garnett, just north of I-244, turned into a shooting. Police say a group showed up to get a repossessed vehicle, one of them pulled a gun on the owner, the owner pulled his, and both the man who started it and the shop owner are dead. The vehicle’s owner took a round in the arm. That’s TPD via FOX23 and KJRH, still an open investigation, not a courtroom finding.

Thursday around 1 a.m., a THC extractor at the Morrow-Gill complex in Sand Springs blew. At least one person dead, another hurt, about 25 neighboring shops damaged. The city’s fire marshal had already told that shop to stop. OSBI and ATF are on it. Tulsa World later said two dead. The story is hazardous extraction (butane-type work) sitting in a strip of other businesses, not the weed.

Tuesday’s GOP runoff: Trump-endorsed Mike Mazzei beat AG Gentner Drummond by about 2,000 votes (roughly 50.3% to 49.7%). Drummond conceded. Mazzei, a former state senator and financial planner who put serious personal money in, faces Democrat Cyndi Munson on Nov. 3. Turnout was weak. SQ 846 (voter ID) passed. SQ 844 (more legislative freedom on property-tax reimbursements) failed.

Stitt, lame duck, tried to put a constitutional convention on an April 6, 2027 special ballot by executive proclamation (State Question 848). The constitution says voters should get that question at least every 20 years. Last time was 1970, and they said no (as they did in 1950 and 1926). Current AG Drummond and former AG Drew Edmondson say a governor cannot put questions on the ballot. That’s the Legislature or the people. NonDoc has the cleanest account. Incentive: the House passed a convention bill this spring and the Senate killed it over who would be the delegates. This is a power play around a blocked bill, not a vote already scheduled. Expect a lawsuit.

The Inola aluminum fight moved today. Trump’s DOJ filed a statement of interest in Drummond’s suit against the $4 billion Oklahoma Primary Aluminum plant (Emirates Global Aluminium 60%, Century Aluminum 40%), calling it a cornerstone of domestic primary aluminum and asking that Drummond’s injunction be denied. Inola’s construction pause was set to lapse today. Locals are worried about air, water, and the power draw. DEQ still has the permit on hold. Fact: Washington just put a thumb on the scale. The jobs vs. neighbors fight is not over.

PSO: a Change.org petition is near 25,000 signatures over bills. That’s noise. The Oklahoma Corporation Commission is the room that matters. You’re on an interim bump of about $11 a month while a settlement that would land around $2.45 waits on a likely mid-October ruling. If the final number is lower, refunds are supposed to show up on the bill automatically.

Faith, order, money, power

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s first Jackson Hole speech landed this morning (primary: federalreserve.gov). PCE inflation is 3.7% over 12 months and 4.1% over six. Unemployment is 4.1%. He will not give forward guidance. He said financial conditions don’t look restrictive, and if he’s not confident inflation is heading to 2%, “we have work to do.” Markets read that as a possible September hike. Trump put him in the chair and wants cuts. That’s the incentive: a president who picked the chair vs. a chair trying to look independent on day 100. The 2-year yield jumped. CME hike odds went from about 35% yesterday to the high 50s. That’s pricing, not a decision.

Economics / geopolitics, plain

The inflation Warsh is staring at is partly the Iran war. Six months in, Hormuz is still a choke point, and energy is doing real work in the price indexes. Treasury’s Operation Economic Outcast (Aug. 24) put secondary-sanctions risk on anyone still touching Iranian oil, gold, crypto, aviation, or shipping, with about 60 designations, a lot of them China and Hong Kong shadow-fleet names. Bessent’s line is pick America or Iran. China is the buyer of that oil and is talking retaliation ahead of a Xi-Trump summit. The mechanism that hits you is energy cost, which shows up in PSO’s case and in the Fed’s PCE print.

Warsh also said more than half of this year’s cap-ex growth is AI buildout, and that he will not let that story drive the current rate decision. Corporate credit is easy. Housing is the sector he named as strained.

Bitcoin

It actually moved. Overnight it tagged about $81,455, a three-month high, then sold off through Warsh’s speech into the high $77,000s. No protocol change. This is rates and liquidity.
