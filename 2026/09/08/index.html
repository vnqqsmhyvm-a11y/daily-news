Tulsa / Oklahoma

Tulsa County’s burn ban hits its stated end today (Sep 8). I found no verified 2026 extension — search hits that say “through Sep 19/21” still resolve to an old 2022 county PDF (same trap as yesterday). Rogers County still banned through Sep 15. Severe drought is the backdrop; check the county before lighting anything.

PSO’s rate case (PUD-2025-000075) is still sitting at the OCC with interim rates in effect and no decision date. Councilor Bellis is still telling ratepayers to put comments on the record.

City Council’s next regular is Sep 16 — the tenant/landlord eviction legal-rep resolution is still queued (~$500k multi-year seed floated; intent and a funding search, not an appropriation).

TPS, citywide budget only (kids aren’t in TPS): Flyer today dug into enrollment ahead of the Oct 19 deadline for Superintendent Johnson’s plan to cut a $12M ops deficit. District data: ~65% average capacity fill; a dozen neighborhood elementaries under 50%; magnets mostly full. Closures not announced; a third-party report recommended closing chronically low-enrollment sites. Community info sessions start Sep 9.

SQ 848 / Apr 6 2027 special is unchanged process: Stitt’s Aug 25 proclamation vs Drummond’s line that the Legislature must pass a bill or joint resolution. Both terms end Jan 11 2027 before the proposed election. Search chatter about a new Mark McBride SCOK filing today — I couldn’t independently confirm it.

National / foreign that touches money and power

Deeds: CENTCOM (via Al-Monitor) says US forces destroyed five IRGC-tied tankers — Derya, Horizon, Kaviz, Charminar, Riesco — after another attempted ballistic-missile attack on a US Navy warship (ship and crew unharmed per CENTCOM; second such attempt in three days). IRGC then warned tanker crews at Kuwait and Bahrain ports to abandon vessels.

Separately, Iran-aligned Houthis hit southern Saudi energy and civilian sites (Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan, Najran). Saudi-led coalition: 73 wounded. Energy ministry: fires and a temporary halt at some facilities. Incentive: Saudi has been shipping west to the Red Sea to bypass Hormuz — that corridor just got hit.

Oil settle (Reuters): Brent $97.92 (+0.9%), WTI $93.03 (+1.7%) — highest Brent close since July 23 (second straight day), highest WTI since June 4. Kpler: 7 commodity vessels through Hormuz Monday vs 8 the day before (pre-war, ~20% of world oil went through the strait). Banks are raising 2026/27 price decks assuming disruption into 2027. Quotas ≠ barrels.

Pump (AAA as of 9/8): regular $4.1514; diesel $5.9012 (yesterday’s $5.9015 is still the record on AAA’s highest-recorded table). Year-ago diesel ~$3.70.

Iranian words, not yet a deed: Ghalibaf says US oil/gas assets in the Gulf “share that exposure” if the US strikes Iranian assets again; Rezaei restates a maritime exclusion zone from the US blockade line through Hormuz. Negotiations still stalled after the Aug 17 MOU deadline.

Plain English

Hormuz choke plus Houthi hits on Saudi Red Sea-side facilities = refined-product crunch (diesel), higher US pumps, and FedWatch ~60% chance of a +25bp hike at the Sep 15–16 FOMC — sticky fuel inflation stacked on a strong payrolls print. Who pays: US consumers and anyone leveraged to diesel first; Iran’s own trapped barrels and ~70% IMF inflation estimate are the other side of the same squeeze.
