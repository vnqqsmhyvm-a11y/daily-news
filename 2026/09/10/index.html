Tulsa / Oklahoma

TPS held the first community meeting Wednesday on how to cut at least $12M by the Oct 19 superintendent plan. On the table: salary freezes, fewer counselors, athletics/fine arts cuts, bus cuts (SPED transport spared, per officials quoted via Tulsa World), and possible closures/consolidations. The room’s main fear was larger class sizes. More sessions run through Oct 14. Citywide fiscal only — kids aren’t in TPS, but a district-wide ops hole this size is the beat. (Public Radio Tulsa)

Burn ban still through Sep 21. PSO rate case PUD-2025-000075: still no final OCC order; interim rates since Jul 1; ALJ report since Aug 21. Same open question as yesterday — interim path closer to ~+$25/mo vs stipulation-with-offsets closer to ~+$11 until the commission actually orders. (OCC process via prior Q&A; no new ECF order found today)

Money / power

Oil ripped. Brent settled $107.63 (+$6.42); WTI $102.48 (+$6.43) — both highest closes since mid-May, steepest one-day jumps in nearly two months. Mechanism: biggest US–Iran shipping attack wave of the six-month war (Iran claimed 10 ship hits near Hormuz after the US sank five IRGC-tied tankers), plus Houthis seizing Mocha and fresh Saudi-facility risk. Trump said the war may outlast the November midterms and floated hitting Pickaxe Mountain near Natanz. Quotas aren’t barrels through Hormuz — the strait still runs far below pre-war. OPEC also cut its 2026 demand-growth forecast again (to 380k bpd). (Reuters)

EIA’s delayed weekly (week ended Sep 4, out today): commercial crude −0.4M to 424.1M (soft vs ~−1.55M expected); SPR −1.2M to 285.4M (−29.6% vs year-ago). Gasoline stocks up 1.3M but still 5% below the five-year; distillate up 2.1M and still 13% below. Refineries at 97.8%. The commercial miss is noise next to the war premium; the SPR draw and tight distillate are the deed. (EIA primary)

Pumps: AAA national regular $4.277; diesel $5.977 — new all-time diesel high (was $5.942 yesterday). Oklahoma regular ~$3.87 on the AAA state banner. (AAA)

10Y (FRED DGS10) 4.83% on Sep 9. Freddie 30Y mortgage (MORTGAGE30US) 6.76% for the week of Sep 10, up from 6.71%. Plain language: war-risk oil is feeding diesel and bond yields; housing credit tightens another notch before tomorrow’s August CPI (8:30am ET), the last big inflation print before the Sep 15–16 FOMC. Futures still lean ~60% toward a +25bp hike — that’s pricing, not a vote.
