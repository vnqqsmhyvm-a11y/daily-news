Thursday, September 3, 2026

Tulsa / Oklahoma

Tulsa’s proposed city-backed legal help for low-income tenants and landlords in eviction cases got pulled off Wednesday’s council agenda at the last minute. District 4’s Laura Bellis is rewriting it to lean harder on mediation before court; it’s expected back Sep 16. Important: even if it passes, it’s a resolution — intent plus a hunt for public/private money — not an ordinance and not a locked appropriation. Bellis has floated a ballpark ~$500k multi-year city seed so philanthropy will put skin in. She cites ~13,000 Tulsa eviction filings a year, only ~3,500 with tenant counsel. Households at or under 200% of federal poverty get priority, especially with kids.

Same calendar: city paperwork sets a Sep 16, 11am sale for $65.66M GO Series 2026A (streets/bridges from the 2019 vote plus parks/community facilities from 2023). That’s a scheduled bond sale, not tonight’s vote.

On your bill: PSO ratepayers are still on the interim hike while OCC sits on docket PUD-2025-000075. Settlement talk has been around ~$11/mo average if approved; final ruling still looking like mid-October. Summer bills jumped on fuel + interim rates + heat — rates didn’t “double,” usage and generation mix did the damage. El Reno’s Athlon Bitcoin-mine nuisance hearing is still Sep 14 (city admits it failed to enforce the 2023 stop-work; gallon figures still split ~3.0–3.8M depending on outlet).

National / foreign (faith, order, money, power)

Hormuz is the incentive fight in plain sight. US officials claim a wartime high — CNN’s sourcing: 40 commercial ships / ~18M barrels under escort Tuesday; Energy Sec. Wright said >17M barrels Monday vs ~20M/day pre-war. Ship trackers tell a thinner story: Kpler counted 5 / 11 / 6 commodity vessels Mon–Wed (~13/day 10-day average); Lloyd’s List ~12/day late August; JMIC still calls traffic “far below baseline” and risk “severe.” Some of the gap may be definition (cargo ships vs every craft under escort, plus dark AIS). Both Washington and Tehran have reason to overclaim control. Meanwhile Kuwait says its air defenses engaged Iranian missiles and drones Thursday — ally under fire while the strait argument runs.

Oil barely moved on the day after a wild week: Brent settled $95.52, WTI $91.30. WTI’s up >9% this week on the renewed strike cycle. Trump’s line Wednesday: he doesn’t think this round lasts “very much longer.” Words.

Economics / geopolitics, plain

Fed Governor Waller (primary remarks today): inflation still “meaningfully above” 2%, but he sees disinflation. If August data keep that trend, he’s inclined to hold the funds rate at the Sep 15–16 meeting; if August reverses, he’d consider a hike. Facts he cited: PCE +3.7% / core +3.3% year-over-year; 3-month core down to 3.05% through July from 4.76% in February; H1 real GDP +1.8% annualized; unemployment 4.1%; payrolls averaging ~60k/month. That’s one governor’s reaction function, not an FOMC vote. Markets eased: 10-year traded around 4.77% after sitting at 4.79% on the official H.15 through Sep 2. August jobs print lands Friday. ISM services August came in hot at 55.4 (consensus 54.1).

Bitcoin

Policy-linked move, not cheerleading: BTC punched back through roughly $80k–$81k Thursday as Waller leaned pause and short liquidations piled up (derivatives desks reporting >$415M in short wipeouts). FedWatch hike odds for September reportedly dropped from the low-60s toward ~50%. Jobs tomorrow and the FOMC in two weeks are the real gates.
