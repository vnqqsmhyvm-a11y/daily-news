Tuesday, September 1, 2026

Tulsa County DA Steve Kunzweiler is pressing the Legislature and OMMA for harder medical-marijuana enforcement after the Aug 27 Sand Springs extraction blast that killed two. Ryan Bassham is charged with two counts of first-degree murder in the commission of a crime; Public Radio Tulsa says he surrendered Saturday and bonded out at $2 million. This afternoon Kunzweiler said he’s in Creek County jail and wants bond denied—NewsOn6 spelled the name Bassam. The DA’s pitch: Oklahoma grows far more than it can smoke, black-market export, foreign crews, and an OMMA that needs real inspection muscle. Separate fact, not his speech: the Sand Springs fire marshal had already told that shop to stop until problems were fixed, and it hadn’t. Law and bodies over slogans—Republican DA asking a Republican Legislature to clean up a voter-legalized industry that still kills people when extraction goes wrong.

At the Capitol, Stitt is still selling State Question 848—an April 6, 2027 special election on whether to call a constitutional convention—as “shall,” not “may,” after 56 years without the every-20-years vote. Today he defended the executive order and proclamation; AG Gentner Drummond’s office calls them “wholly inconsistent with the Oklahoma Constitution” and says only a bill or joint resolution gets a question on the ballot. Senate Pro Tem Lonnie Paxton says Stitt jumped unilaterally while the House/Senate were already fighting over who gets the delegates. Incentive: lame-duck governor forcing a process fight onto the next governor and a court. NonDoc’s process file still stands: Art. 24 §2, three prior convention questions failed (1926/1950/1970), and 1994’s attempt to repeal the 20-year ask also failed.

Tulsa money: ONEOK’s Aug 31 8-K locks the paperwork. Apollo affiliate puts up $9 billion for Class B units in a new HoldCo; ONEOK uses that to buy Brazos Midland gathering/processing for $4.425 billion cash and knock down about $5 billion of debt via tenders. Class B is nonvoting, structurally junior to debt, initially takes 15% of OpCo cash-flow from operations against a capped ~7% IRR. Closing window around mid-September for the equity piece; Brazos still needs HSR. Private credit writing a long check into Tulsa midstream so the company can buy Permian pipe without flooding the common-equity market.

Abroad and in the bond pit: CENTCOM, as quoted by Reuters, struck IRGC targets Tuesday—air defense, radar, maritime/mine gear, comms—after weekend fire over Hormuz mine-laying and Iranian missiles at U.S. bases in Jordan. Iranian Red Crescent says five dead and dozens hurt at a wedding near Sirik, including a child per Mehr; U.S. side hasn’t confirmed those numbers. Iran claims heavy U.S. deaths in Jordan; U.S. officials told Reuters no casualties on first look. Jordan says it knocked down most of the inbound missiles. Incentive on both sides: choke or reopen the strait that used to move roughly one in five barrels. Brent settled about +$4.16 to $94.65; WTI +$4.46 to $90.22—five-week highs. Japan’s 10-year JGB touched 3% for the first time since 1996; U.S. 10-year around the high 4.78% area while Bessent talks up growth, yen support, and bigger long-end Treasury buybacks. Higher energy + higher global yields is the macro tape—not a soft-landing lull.

Bitcoin: morning prints near $78k, basically sideways while oil and yields jumped. No protocol or policy move worth a separate beat.

Sports, short: Cards dropped Sunday 5–4 to Pittsburgh, off Monday, open at Dodger Stadium tonight (9:10 CT). Braves’ seven-game streak ended Monday, Giants 7–3; Atlanta at Washington tonight. OU opens Friday vs UTEP; OSU Saturday vs Murray State. Wrestling quiet.
