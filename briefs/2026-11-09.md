**Tulsa and Oklahoma.** A Tulsa Public Schools Suburban carrying seven Webster High students and a staff member home from the state student council convention in Norman collided with a semi on the Turner Turnpike near mile marker 170 around 12:45 p.m. One student died and several were taken to Tulsa hospitals in critical condition, per TPS and the Highway Patrol (KOTV reporting, carried by the Tulsa Flyer). Eastbound lanes were closed through the afternoon. Pray for those families.

Inola smelter: opponents in Protect Inola say they have more than half of the 5,000 signatures needed to petition for a Rogers County grand jury on the project (News On 6, their count, not a filed petition). Gov.-elect Mazzei was the only governor candidate who backed the smelter; he now says he won't let it be built if it can't be done safely. That is a statement, not a condition written anywhere. The live questions are still the incentive package and who pays for the power on the rate base.

Pawnee: the city authorized an electric transmission cost study for a possible data center by Oklahoma AI Ventures in western Pawnee County, paid for by the developer. No commitments yet. The mayor says project-specific grid costs must fall on the developer, not existing customers. Worth watching whether that promise survives into a signed contract, since Pawnee runs its own electric utility.

State: certification of the Nov. 3 results is set for Tuesday at the State Election Board; returns are still unofficial. Mazzei's transition is in the planning stage only: he has floated an office of state finance and fiscal responsibility and quarterly budget-versus-actual reports from every agency, with cabinet and literacy-director picks promised by late November. He wants income-tax elimination and property-tax relief for seniors and veterans, funded by cuts he hasn't named yet, and the new 75–26 House sits one vote short of the three-fourths needed to raise revenue without a vote of the people (Oklahoma Politics Today). Watch for the line items.

Also: MetroLink started an eight-week Axon body-camera pilot for its paratransit drivers and supervisors (Nov. 9–Jan. 2). The state Securities Department launched a "Fraud-Safe" training program, citing $130 million-plus in reported Oklahoma fraud losses last year. Tulsa City Council's next regular meeting is Nov. 18, when the Peak on the Parkway TIF districts and the Vision economic-development reshuffle come up.

**National and foreign.** Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) filed eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and says he will force a House vote. The text charges violations of three sections of the War Powers Resolution over Iran, continuing hostilities after Congress passed H. Con. Res. 86 directing withdrawal, an unauthorized Yemen operation, lethal boat strikes he counts at 243-plus deaths, the Maduro seizure, and retaliation against Sen. Mark Kelly (Massie's own release; the charges are his, not findings). The incentive: Democrats take both chambers in January, and Massie is openly telling the President to swap Hegseth now while a Republican Senate can still confirm a replacement. Separately, Trump named Stephen Miller chief of staff and, on 60 Minutes, blamed the midterm losses partly on the war and gas prices.

Iran: after meeting Qatar's prime minister at the White House, Trump said the war is "militarily effectively over" and renewed strikes are "quite unlikely." No deal was announced. Hours earlier Iranian officials said U.S. strikes hit Qeshm Island again overnight (Washington didn't directly confirm), and the Revolutionary Guard claimed it destroyed navigation aids in the southern strait so that no "unauthorized" ship transited. Iranian state TV counts 11 tankers attacked in 10 days. Tehran's "two-day plan" amounts to lifting the U.S. port blockade and some sanctions first, then talks, which Rubio called "ridiculous." Words on both sides are soft; the deeds (strikes, tanker hits, a blockade) haven't changed.

**Money and markets.** Oil fell hard on Trump's no-strikes line: Brent settled $92.57 (down 5.9%), WTI $86.85 (down 4.4%), per Reuters and CNBC. Stocks had their best day since April, S&P 500 up 2.35% to 7,549.94, Dow up 1,104 to 52,389.75, Nasdaq up 2.7%, clawing back part of last week's post-election AI selloff. Nvidia fell 4.2% to its lowest close since April after reports its board is investigating CEO Jensen Huang's ties to a woman U.S. intelligence reportedly flagged as a Chinese agent (reporting, not a finding). The 10-year sat near 4.88%; gold rose about 1.8%. Note that a single day's oil drop came from a presidential quote, not from more barrels moving through Hormuz.

Tomorrow at 7:30 a.m. Central: October CPI. September ran 3.7% year over year, and futures still lean toward a December Fed hike. Bond markets close Wednesday for Veterans Day.

**Bitcoin.** About $75,000, its lowest close since August, after failing above $80,000 last Wednesday. U.S. spot ETFs lost $1.28 billion last week, the most since late June, with BlackRock's IBIT accounting for $1.21 billion; 2026 ETF flows are now negative year to date (SoSoValue data via CoinDesk). That cuts against the "rotation out of AI into bitcoin" story and the safe-haven pitch for now. Flows, not the protocol, are driving this.
