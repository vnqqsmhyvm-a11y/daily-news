**Tulsa and Oklahoma.**

**Mazzei rounds out his inner circle.** Governor-elect Mike Mazzei named former state Rep. Eric Roberts secretary of state and former Sen. Marty Quinn senior policy adviser. He also named three working-group chairs: Fire Chief Ray Driskell for public safety, Dr. Chris Sudduth for health care, and Greg Arend for fiscal responsibility. His transition team grew to include former Attorney General John O'Connor, former Reps. John Bennett and Jake Merrick, and Rep. Jay Stegall (FOX23, from the transition release). Roberts spent 34 years in commercial real estate. So far these are all personnel moves. The first real test is still the income-tax bill he has promised to send the Legislature the first week of February.

**Tulsa: the big items are parked until Dec. 2.** At its Nov. 18 meeting, the council pushed three decisions to its Dec. 2 meeting: the data center moratorium extension, the Peak on the Parkway tax-increment plan, and homelessness funding (Oklahoma Record meeting record). City offices close Thursday and Friday for Thanksgiving.

**National and foreign.**

**Treasury goes after Iran's money pipes while the strait burns.** *Confirmed (Treasury press release; OFAC actions list, Nov. 23):* OFAC sanctioned more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies, and officials. They include Mellat Exchange, Fashki Exchange, and the Iran Center for Exchange of Currency and Gold, which Treasury says handles tens of billions of dollars a year in trade. Most of the front companies are in Dubai, with others in Hong Kong and Turkey. A FinCEN analysis released alongside the sanctions found that 52% of the flagged funds, $7.6 billion, went to recipients registered in the UAE. That's a U.S. partner, and secondary sanctions now point straight at its banks. *Claimed by Iran, unverified:* the IRGC navy says three more tankers hit mines and caught fire Monday, two of them "under extensive US military air escort." No independent authority has confirmed any of Iran's mine claims, and the maritime security center's latest update is still Sunday's (Update 116). Washington's strategy is to cut off Iran's money and escort ships instead of negotiating. Tehran's is to make every passage feel like a gamble.

**Security watch.**

**Nvidia: Huang is back, and the President says he made the deal.** *Confirmed (Nvidia release):* the board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO and director, effective immediately, less than two weeks after he resigned. The board said it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and is confident his return "poses no national security risk." The independent committee looking into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort will "proceed to conclusion." Nvidia also created a board committee for national security and export controls and is hiring its first Chief Compliance Officer. Colette Kress goes back to being CFO. *On the record:* President Trump told CNBC "I made the deal" and said he talked with board members beforehand. On Truth Social he said the government had no evidence of unlawful conduct and attributed that to Attorney General Todd Blanche. *Confirmed (House joint statement):* the chairmen of the China, Intelligence, and Foreign Affairs committees, all Republicans, said the reinstatement does "not erase the national security threats." Huang and general counsel Tim Teter have confirmed their Dec. 1 and Dec. 2 interviews. *Still open:* the DOJ's Megaspeed probe, the SEC inquiry (reported, never confirmed), and the California AG's subpoena. **Why it matters:** the White House now owns this outcome. It said there's no evidence while its own investigations are still running, and a day earlier Treasury was publicly asking how high up the compliance failure went. Watch whether House Republicans keep pushing against their own President on Dec. 1.

FAA: ten days after the ground stop, the root cause the agency promised still hasn't been released.

**Economy and markets.**

**Markets now treat a December rate hike as nearly certain.** Futures put about a 98% chance on a quarter-point Fed hike at the Dec. 8–9 meeting, up from about 88% Friday (CME FedWatch, via CNBC). The 2-year Treasury yield ended at 4.63% and the 10-year at 4.92% (Treasury daily yield curve). New York Fed President John Williams, a permanent voter on rate decisions, said Monday there is "still work to do" and that getting inflation back to 2% is "100% essential," but that the Fed should act "over time." He blamed the Iran war, demand from the AI buildout, and tariffs, and said the tariff effects "have largely abated." Those are words. The action comes Dec. 9. For anyone carrying a card balance or shopping for a loan, borrowing costs are rising, not falling.

**Oil fell despite the strait.** Brent settled down $3.20 at $89.37 and WTI down $3.24 at $80.17, Brent's lowest close since Aug. 31. Libya's Sharara pipeline restarted and more Gulf crude is getting out by other routes (Reuters). JPMorgan sees crude in the high $70s in the first half of 2027 because it expects a supply glut. A market that shrugs off claimed mine strikes is betting the escorts and the bypass routes hold.
