**Tulsa / Oklahoma**

Federal DOJ stepped into Attorney General Gentner Drummond’s suit against the proposed Inola primary aluminum smelter (Oklahoma Primary Aluminum / EGA orbit). In Friday filings it asks the court to deny his preliminary injunction (or pause the case until DEQ finishes the air permit). Framing: national security, domestic primary aluminum, planned capacity over 750,000 metric tons/year, and a potential federal award up to about $500 million. Drummond’s side is environment and public health, including projected emissions. Separately, DEQ’s permit tracker still shows Tier III major-source construction in technical review, status **Awaiting Applicant Response** (no construction permit issued). So: federal industrial-policy muscle vs state AG, while the real gate is still DEQ paper and the rate-base question of who pays for the megawatts. (News On 6 on the filing; DEQ tracker for permit status.)

PSO’s rate case is still the live bill hit. OCC ALJ Kenneth Behrens recommended a path that could leave average residential increases roughly in the $9.64–$12.09 range, expand/extend riders, and (with PSO not signed on) approve a large-load tariff for customers over 75 MW — data-center territory. Commissioner Todd Hiett’s best guess to the Flyer: commissioners get the case late September, final ruling mid-October; OG&E/Google special-contract hearings start Sept 9 and could slip the calendar. Meanwhile you’re still paying the ~$11 interim; any overage vs the final order is supposed to refund. Reminder from your voting lens: regulated utilities earn on capital they build — “growing demand” is the lever. Who profits from that buildout is the story, not the CEO letter. (Tulsa Flyer; OCC docket PUD 2025-000075 — ECF PDF not opened this run.)

On the November 3 ballot: City Council put a **hotel guest tax** hike from 5% to 9.9% (hotels and short-term rentals only). If it passes, effective Jan 1, 2027, and the city’s pitch is $80 million in revenue bonds for BOK Center / Arvest Convention Center work (part of a claimed ~$128 million package with reserves, Oak View Group money, and Improve Our Tulsa 3). Sold as “other people’s money” — tourists, not residents — for venue debt and tourism marketing. That’s a real local prop to verify on the OK Voter Portal sample ballot when you’re closer in; I’m not telling you how to vote. (Tulsa City Council primary post.)

**National / money / power**

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s first Jackson Hole keynote (Friday) is the macro deed of the day — words, not a rate vote. From the Fed’s own text: PCE inflation 3.7% over 12 months and 4.1% over six; summer prints better than expected but “do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved”; prices should be the Fed’s predominant focus; “the responsibility for 65 months of sustained, elevated inflation sits squarely with the central bank.” He rejected routine forward guidance (“committed to a discipline, not to a decision”), said he would be hard-pressed to call broad financial conditions restrictive (easy credit spreads, strong cap-ex, AI buildout), and put AI productivity/jobs on a task force with no near-term policy weight. Funds rate was left at 3.50–3.75% at the July FOMC (with dissenters for a hike, per secondary coverage). So: hawkish diagnosis, no hike yet. (federalreserve.gov speech.)
