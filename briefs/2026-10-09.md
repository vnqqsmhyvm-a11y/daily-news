**Tulsa and Oklahoma.** Voter registration for the Nov 3 general election closed today, so anyone in the house who isn't registered has missed it. Absentee ballot requests are due 15 days before the election, and early voting runs Oct 28–31. Oklahoma Voice reports the state Ethics Commission is investigating Rep. Danny Williams (R-Seminole). He's accused of using his office to help a for-profit company and two nonprofits, including selling a product to the Seminole County Jail. For now those are allegations, not findings, and the case is separate from a House inquiry into him. The Oklahoma Indian Gaming Association says prediction markets like Kalshi are cutting into the tribes' exclusive gaming rights. Tribal gaming fees paid the state more than $221 million last budget year, and sports betting talks at the Capitol have stalled. I only saw the headlines on a few Tulsa World stories:
- The attorney general cited the Inola aluminum smelter in a challenge to the federal ozone rule.
- TPS has no plan yet for the buildings that school closures will leave empty.
- Medicaid recipients are being told to check their eligibility before the new work rules start.

City Council meets Wednesday.

**Money and the world.** The 10-year Treasury yield ended near 5.25%, the 2-year at 4.80% and the 30-year at 5.63%, and long-term rates are close to their highest in decades. Thursday's 30-year bond auction drew solid demand. The job market is weaker than it looks: September added only 29,000 jobs, and July and August were revised down a combined 60,000. Consumer sentiment fell to 46.3 while people's inflation expectations rose. That mix of slow growth and rising prices is in the data, not just a forecast. Markets see about an 18% chance of a Fed rate hike on Oct 28 and about 82% by December. Gold bounced back to about $4,190. Oil eased, with Brent around $103 and WTI around $91, after Trump said there would be no strikes on Iran before the midterms. The same week, though, Washington sanctioned 17 more ships tied to Iranian oil, and threats to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz are up this month. Cheaper gas before Nov 3 helps the White House. The consumer and producer price reports come out next week.
