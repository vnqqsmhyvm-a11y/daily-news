**Tulsa and Oklahoma.**

**Feds tally a 41-day Tulsa gun-and-drug sweep.** The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of Oklahoma says Operation Get Your Kicks, run Oct. 8 through Nov. 17 and led by ATF with DEA, Marshals, HSI, the Tulsa County Sheriff and Tulsa police, produced 23 arrests, 122 guns and about 126 pounds of meth (News On 6, from the U.S. Attorney's announcement). Of the 23 arrests, 13 people face federal criminal charges and 11 were arrested on federal immigration charges. Prosecutors tied some of the trafficking to the Juárez Cartel, and some defendants face material-support-to-terrorism charges, possible now that cartels carry a terrorist designation. Agents chose where to work using the national ballistics network (NIBIN) and crime data. A press-conference tally is not the same as convictions. The number to watch is how many of the 13 federal cases end in a conviction, and how many of the 23 arrests were immigration cases, not violent-crime cases.

**Education Department won't say how many schools can still serve a third meal.** The state ended the federally funded Expanded Learning Time supper program on Sept. 30, after school had already started. It told districts to switch to after-school meal funding, which requires at least 30 minutes of structured activity and a bus home. A total of 118 districts have applied, including 54 of the 79 that used to serve supper, but the agency won't say how many it approved (Oklahoma Voice, from agency records). Peggs Public Schools says its third meal is just gone, and local churches are sending snack packs home on Fridays. The agency promised "kids will not go hungry" but won't publish the number that would show it.

**Recruiting Texans is still just talk.** Gov. Stitt and Gov.-elect Mazzei are pitching Oklahoma to Texans unhappy with the Democratic sweep there (The Oklahoman [paywalled — headline only]). So far that's an invitation, not a policy. Mazzei's income-tax plan, due in the first week of February, is the part with a dollar figure attached.

**Holiday week.** Tulsa International expects about 80,000 travelers this week, and gas in Tulsa averaged $2.99 Wednesday morning (News On 6). On Dec. 2 the City Council takes up the data center moratorium extension and the Peak on the Parkway TIF.

**National and foreign.**

**Washington rebuilds the struck-down tariffs under another law.** *Confirmed (USTR):* Trade Representative Jamieson Greer ruled that "structural excess capacity" in 14 economies is unreasonable under Section 301 and proposed tariffs. The 14 are China, the EU, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, India, Vietnam, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Cambodia and Bangladesh. Proposed rates mostly run 15% to 20%, with Vietnam up to 35% and India up to 25%. Mexico and Norway weren't ruled on. Greer told CNBC they'd mostly "top up a few percentage points" on existing deals, wouldn't take effect for months, and that China's 20% lands on top of the first-term 25%, for about 45% total. **The incentive:** the Supreme Court threw out the "liberation day" tariffs, and the administration opened these cases weeks later, in March. Section 301 is the legal workaround. The listed causes include "financial repression" and "wage suppression" abroad. That's a fair criticism of other countries' policies, but Americans pay the tariff all the same: importers pay it first, then customers. Markets took it as one more reason inflation stays high.

**Gulf: drones over Saudi Arabia's biggest refinery, more ships hit.** The Houthis say they hit Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's largest refinery and an export terminal that can load about 6.5 million barrels a day, along with Riyadh's financial district. *Confirmed (Saudi-led coalition):* four drones were shot down over Ras Tanura and debris fell, with no casualties in the first assessment. The coalition did not say the refinery was hit. Online footage appeared to show smoke at the site (The National). Brent jumped 3.5% on the news. *UKMTO:* a tanker leaving Hormuz was hit by an "unidentified projectile" and had an engine-room fire, with no injuries reported. Separately, an explosion went off near a tanker 50 nautical miles west of Dubai, with no damage and all crew safe. Iran again says ships hit its mines, and again nobody has confirmed that. Treasury Secretary Bessent posted that Iran hasn't loaded a single tanker since the blockade began in August. That's a claim on X, not data. Iran's rial hit a record low of about 2.86 million to the dollar on Tehran's open market (Iran International).

**Security watch.**

**Huang "exonerated" by an investigation that, per Reuters, never happened.** *Reported (Reuters, three anonymous sources):* the Justice Department never opened a formal investigation of Jensen Huang before Trump pushed to bring him back. AG Blanche based his "no evidence" conclusion on ongoing probes of two Nvidia customers, Super Micro and Megaspeed. Nvidia wasn't a target in those and cooperated. There was also no counterintelligence probe of Huang himself, even though U.S. spy agencies had been collecting on the former Megaspeed executive he's alleged to have had an affair with. Reuters says the conclusion "is subject to change" as those probes continue. *On the record:* Vice President Vance said Wednesday that DOJ and national-security officials told him Huang was "under no investigation." On Monday, Trump told CNBC, "They did a pretty good investigation, and they felt that he, himself, was exonerated." Both can't be true. **Why it matters:** the CEO of the company that makes America's most export-controlled chips got cleared without anyone actually examining him, and the White House that cleared him also claims credit for the deal. Congress is where questions get asked under oath, starting with the House interviews Dec. 1–2 and Senate Banking on Dec. 3.

**FAA: a suspected cause, and the FAA says the contractor's work was the contractor's job.** *Reported (CNN, one source familiar with the investigation):* investigators recreated the Nov. 13 failure in a lab. They traced it to an overlooked setting on the new El Dorado, Ark., router that interacted badly with old routers the FAA is retiring. They believe software patches will stop a repeat, and a report is being prepared. *On the record (FAA statement to CNN):* "L3 Harris owns and is responsible for maintaining" the network, and FAA staff "never certify or sign off on the internal workmanship" of an L3Harris router install. *Union claim (PASS president David Spero):* a Trump administration directive removed FAA approval of contractor maintenance timing, and only four FAA liaisons coordinate that work nationwide. In 2009, a misconfiguration by a Harris Corp. technician caused a similar nationwide outage. **The incentives:** the FAA is pushing blame onto the contractor, the union wants its approval role back, and Secretary Duffy is asking Congress for another $50 billion. The official root cause still hasn't been released, 12 days on.

**Economy and markets.**

**Inflation is speeding up, the economy is running hot, and the Fed is expected to hike again.** *BEA:* October PCE prices rose 0.3% for the month and 3.8% from a year ago, up from 3.7%. Core was 3.3%. Income rose 0.7%, but inflation-adjusted spending was flat, and the saving rate rose to 4.1%. Third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3% annualized from 3.4%, and private domestic demand grew 5.8%. Paychecks are rising, but they buy about the same amount of stuff, and nothing here says policy is tight. Markets put the odds of a quarter-point hike at the Dec. 8–9 Fed meeting above 95% (Kitco, citing futures). Even so, the 10-year yield fell to about 4.86% as money moved into bonds for safety (CNBC). That leaves roughly one point of yield above 3.8% inflation, a thin cushion for savers.

**Stocks:** the Dow rose 164 points to 50,182.95. The S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq fell 0.62% to 23,585.80. Early in the day the Nasdaq 100 was briefly 20% below its record, and the Nasdaq is down 7.3% for November. Nvidia fell 3.9% (Reuters). **Oil:** January Brent settled at $94.61 and WTI at $83.68. U.S. crude stocks rose 1.1 million barrels to 418.9 million (EIA, via Reuters), and OPEC+ sets its 2027 output policy Nov. 29. **Gold** recovered to about $3,576 after dipping below $3,450, and silver to about $41 (Kitco). Markets are closed Thursday and close early Friday.
