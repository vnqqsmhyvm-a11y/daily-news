**Tulsa and Oklahoma.** The Peak on the Parkway tax-increment plan gets its first public hearing at Wednesday's 5 p.m. City Council meeting. The filed agenda shows four new increment districts (three capturing both property and sales tax, one property-only), with the Tulsa Authority for Economic Opportunity running the money and a second hearing Dec. 2. Earlier committee coverage put it at about $200 million on 40 acres. That figure comes from a summary of the committee video, not a city document, so check it against the project plan before taking it as settled. The question to ask is how much future county, library and school property-tax growth gets diverted to pay project costs, and for how long. The same agenda has a $65,000 judgment payment from the Sinking Fund in Moore v. City of Tulsa, plus closed-door sessions on legal representation for two officers and on the long-running Crutcher estate case.

Rogers State University says its regents authorized taking over downtown's Atlas School, the coding school the George Kaiser Family Foundation started in 2020. The foundation would give RSU Atlas's assets and keep paying the lease and operating costs, pending approvals. That's a public university picking up a foundation program while the foundation keeps funding it. Watch whether the state ever takes on that operating cost.

The new Legislature is sworn in this week: senators Monday at 2 p.m., House members Tuesday at 10 a.m. Tulsa International was still running cancellations into the weekend after Friday's ground stop, so check your flight before you leave.

**Faith, order, money, power.** Iran fired on Bahrain for the second time in three days. The Revolutionary Guard says it sent six drones at a US air-defense radar at Riffa Air Base Sunday morning. Bahrain's Defence Force says its air defenses stopped several Iranian "aerial attacks," and Bahrain's Interior Ministry sent residents to shelters twice. CENTCOM hadn't commented on Sunday's attack by this evening. On Friday's attack, it said it shot down two drones with no damage or injuries. At sea, the Guard now claims a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels sank after hitting a mine on an "unauthorized" southern route. CENTCOM calls the mine claims false and says the main lanes are cleared. No vessel has been named. UK maritime monitors (UKMTO) say they're aware of an Indian report of an attack on a tanker in the Gulf with the crew safe, but it's unclear whether that's the same ship. Reuters reports, citing three sources, that Trump has told advisers he may hold off on new strikes unless Iran or the Houthis provoke the US. Incentive: Tehran wants insurers to believe the escorted route is unsafe, and Washington wants them to believe it's clear. Neither has offered a ship name, a hull or a photo. Separately, the White House and Kremlin say Trump, Xi and Putin will meet Thursday in Shenzhen on the sidelines of APEC, the first three-way meeting of the three presidents. Arms control and Iran are reportedly on the agenda. Ukraine reportedly isn't.

**Security watch.** *FAA ground stop, new facts.* The FAA's Sunday Q&A narrows the cause to two sites: a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where contractor L3Harris had just installed a new router, and a radar site in Amarillo, Texas. Data flooding from those sites destabilized the FAA's legacy network. L3Harris cut both sites off, and the network stabilized. The FAA has since turned off the routers' broadcasting function on about 60 similar routers as a precaution. Secretary Duffy said Sunday that both routers are at L3Harris's Florida lab for diagnostics, the FBI has offered help, and "we think it was the installation or the router that was the bad actor," with no sign of a foreign cyberattack. He added that the routers that faltered on the receiving end were bought in 2001 and obsolete by 2006. Reported (Wall Street Journal): the new unit is a Juniper router, 17 of 22 high-altitude centers lost communications, and Juniper's owner HPE says its devices "did not fail." Still open: was it bad hardware, a bad configuration, or a bad install? Speculation: sabotage, which nothing official supports. Incentive: DOT says Congress's $12.5 billion for modernization was only a down payment, and every version of this story strengthens its ask for more. The contractor and the router maker each have reasons to point at the other.

**Economics.** Futures reopened calm Sunday evening: Nasdaq contracts up 0.6% and S&P up 0.2% after Nvidia's worst week since April 2025, which erased more than half a trillion dollars of its market value (Bloomberg). Brent is about $95 and US crude about $88, both slightly lower despite the Gulf attacks. The 10-year Treasury yield is 5.14%, and the dollar sits near its strongest since May 2025. With wholesale prices up 6.5% from a year ago and Fed officials like Kansas City's Jeff Schmid saying there's more work to do, a December hike is still the base case. In plain terms: money is getting more expensive, not cheaper, and that hits mortgages and car loans first.

**Bitcoin.** Bitcoin fell to about $62,000 over the weekend, its lowest since July, before recovering to about $65,000 Sunday evening. Rising yields and a strong dollar are doing the damage. A "hedge" that falls with tech stocks when rates rise is acting like a high-risk tech stock. It's a price move, not a protocol or policy change.

The Sooners are 6–4. Oklahoma State lost 52–27 to No. 10 Texas Tech, which effectively ends its Big 12 title hopes.
