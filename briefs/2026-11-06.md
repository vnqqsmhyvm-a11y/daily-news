**Tulsa and Oklahoma.** The City of Tulsa closed the sixth field season of the 1921 graves investigation at Oaklawn Cemetery. Experts documented 47 previously unrecorded burials this season; forensic work identified gunshot wounds in two of the people exhumed, bringing the running total with gunshot trauma across all seasons to 10. Nine individuals this season met the search criteria (adults in unadorned containers and/or trauma). DNA and further analysis continue; a preliminary summary is expected in the coming months. That is the city’s own press release, not a new identification of named massacre victims beyond the one already confirmed (C.L. Daniel).

The Oklahoma Turnpike Authority board approved statewide and Gilcrease Expressway toll schedules effective Jan. 5, 2027. The statewide change is sized for about a 6% increase in overall toll revenue and is the second ACCESS Oklahoma bond-related adjustment; staff said the board may revisit every two years. On Gilcrease, PikePass passenger trips rise 15 cents (~7%) — the first rate increase requested for that five-mile corridor after federal work deferred earlier 3% steps. Vehicle classes compress from five to three (axle-based). Dollars on the road, not speeches.

Gov.-elect Mike Mazzei, in a News On 6 interview Friday, said cabinet names and a statewide literacy director shortlist come toward the end of November, floated 90-day agency budget-versus-actual reports, and said that if the Inola aluminum smelter moves forward he would bring in a third-party Clean Air Act evaluator — while still backing the project that outgoing AG Gentner Drummond is fighting and incoming AG Jon Echols says he will keep litigating. Those are transition words. County election boards were set to certify Friday at 5 p.m.; the State Election Board still labels Nov. 3 results unofficial until county certification and the Nov. 10 state meeting. No rebuild of Tuesday’s ballot here.

**National / foreign.** UK Maritime Trade Operations Warning 197-26 (posted Nov. 6) reports a tanker attack: explosion at the port-side waterline at 22:00 UTC on Nov. 5, about 16 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman, outbound through Hormuz. All crew safe and accounted for; the unnamed ship continued under its own power. Separate from the Nov. 4 Velos Sapphire strike north of Qatar (UKMTO 196-26), where one seafarer died. Deed, not a speech: another hull hit while oil markets price a possible diplomatic off-ramp after the midterms.

**Economics / geopolitics.** The October jobs print was the number. BLS (primary): nonfarm payrolls +165,000 — well above the ~65,000 consensus that had been carried into the week — with the unemployment rate at 4.1% (from 4.2%). Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month to $38.00 (+3.1% year over year). August was revised to +186,000 and September to +60,000 (+84,000 combined). Gains concentrated in professional and business services (+70,000), health care (+42,000), transportation and warehousing (+25,000), and manufacturing (+19,000). Soft under the headline: people working part time for economic reasons rose 339,000 to 4.8 million, and the broader U-6 underutilization rate moved to 7.9%. That is still a labor market the Fed can call tight enough to hike.

Markets priced it that way. CME FedWatch December 25-basis-point hike odds climbed to about 77% from the high-60s Thursday. The 10-year Treasury yield settled near 4.86%. Equities closed mixed after Thursday’s rout: Dow 51,285.73 (−0.46%), S&P 500 7,376.86 (+0.31%), Nasdaq 24,579.42 (+1.09%). Oil barely moved on the day — WTI $90.83 (+31¢), Brent $98.32 (+3¢) — after a roughly 13% weekly washout as traders weighed ceasefire talk against ongoing tanker hits. EIA’s weekly (week ended Oct. 30, out Thursday): commercial crude ex-SPR 409.8 million barrels (−0.87), distillate 99.3 million (−3.57), SPR 282.2 million (−0.40). Distillate keeps the real squeeze; the weekly crude draw alone does not.
