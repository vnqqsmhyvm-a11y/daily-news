**Tulsa and Oklahoma.** A quiet Sunday, so the useful items are ones on the calendar. Tulsa County's election board certified local results Friday evening (Tulsa World headline; story paywalled). The State Election Board certifies state and federal races Tuesday, Nov. 10; its results page still reads "unofficial," and no contest or recount filing turned up today.

The arithmetic that matters for your wallet: unofficial returns leave the Oklahoma House at 75 Republicans to 26 Democrats, down from 81–20. The last flip was an 89-vote Democratic win in Lawton's House District 64 (Oklahoma Politics Today, citing State Election Board returns). Republicans keep two-thirds, but the state constitution requires three-fourths of both chambers (76 House votes) to pass a revenue-raising bill without a statewide vote. Any tax increase the 61st Legislature wants now needs at least one Democrat, or goes to the ballot. The Senate is unchanged at 40–8. Gov.-elect Mike Mazzei has named his brother Dan to chair the transition (Oklahoma Voice).

At City Hall, the next money votes are set. The council's economic-development committee heard two items Nov. 4 that go to the full council Nov. 18 with a public hearing Dec. 2 (per a meeting summary and transcript posted by Open Publica; the agenda PDFs were not opened tonight). The first is "Peak on the Parkway," a $200 million North Tulsa project on 40 vacant acres that would create four tax-increment financing (TIF) districts for medical offices, a grocery, restaurants and housing. Councilors praised it. Nobody in the summary asked how much future property tax the four districts would divert, or for how long. That is the question to ask before Dec. 2. The second is $6.594 million from leftover 2016 Vision sales-tax economic-development money: a $750,000 floodgate at the Mohawk golf course, $1.5 million for an airport child-care center, $1.5 million for an east-side business incubator, $450,000 of public parking for the private Irving on Brookside development, and a $1 million after-school pilot. Councilors held the pilot back over sole-source contracting concerns until a competitive process is spelled out. Separately, Woodward Park's north end reopened after a $6.855 million job that replaced a nearly 100-year-old stormwater pipe and rebuilt four ponds (KTUL via Today in Tulsa).

**National / foreign.** President Trump told CNN by phone Sunday that new strikes on Iran are "highly unlikely" to be necessary if Tehran signs "a very strong agreement," and added that the U.S. has picked 38 bridges "that we can knock down in literally one hour" (Reuters). That is words. Here is what was done this weekend:

- CENTCOM said an aircraft fired on the stern of a St. Kitts-flagged cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman Saturday and disabled it for trying to run the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports.
- CENTCOM also stood up "Task Force Iron Gate," based in Tampa, to choke Iran's overland and Caspian trade.
- The IRGC navy says three tankers caught fire after hitting mines on "unauthorized routes" Sunday. Reuters could not confirm it. The Institute for the Study of War reads the near-daily mine claims as an effort to scare shippers into asking Tehran's permission.
- UK Maritime Trade Operations separately logged a tanker struck by a projectile outbound Saturday and another tanker fire off Khasab.
- The Houthis hit Saudi Arabia again. Riyadh says debris from an attempted attack damaged a Saudi Aramco office building in the capital's financial district, with no injuries.
- Qatar denied reports that talks had collapsed.

Iran's stated price, per a senior Iranian official speaking to Reuters, is sanctions relief, frozen assets and reparations before nuclear talks. Washington says nuclear issues come first. Neither side has moved in public.

Watch the traffic numbers too. Lloyd's List Intelligence puts Hormuz traffic at about 20 percent of pre-war levels (via The National). Thursday's UKMTO weekly implied roughly 35 percent. Different counting methods, and nobody can independently verify the U.S.-escorted transits. A third carrier, the Theodore Roosevelt, is reported due in the region this week. The incentive on both sides is to look ready to fight while a Qatari-brokered deal is priced in.

**Economics / geopolitics.** Brent reopened Sunday evening near $98.06 and slipped to about $97.50 in the first hour. More than a dozen Fed speeches are scheduled. Weigh the votes, not the speeches.
