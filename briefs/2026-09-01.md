Tulsa County DA Steve Kunzweiler is pressing the Legislature and OMMA for harder medical-marijuana enforcement after the Aug 27 Sand Springs extraction blast that killed two. Ryan Bassham is charged with two counts of first-degree murder in the commission of a crime; Public Radio Tulsa says he surrendered Saturday and bonded out at $2 million. This afternoon Kunzweiler said he’s in Creek County jail and wants bond denied—NewsOn6 spelled the name Bassam. The DA’s pitch: Oklahoma grows far more than it can smoke, black-market export, foreign crews, and an OMMA that needs real inspection muscle. Separate fact, not his speech: the Sand Springs fire marshal had already told that shop to stop until problems were fixed, and it hadn’t. Law and bodies over slogans—Republican DA asking a Republican Legislature to clean up a voter-legalized industry that still kills people when extraction goes wrong.

At the Capitol, Stitt is still selling State Question 848—an April 6, 2027 special election on whether to call a constitutional convention—as “shall,” not “may,” after 56 years without the every-20-years vote. Today he defended the executive order and proclamation; AG Gentner Drummond’s office calls them “wholly inconsistent with the Oklahoma Constitution” and says only a bill or joint resolution gets a question on the ballot. Senate Pro Tem Lonnie Paxton says Stitt jumped unilaterally while the House/Senate were already fighting over who gets the delegates. Incentive: lame-duck governor forcing a process fight onto the next governor and a court. NonDoc’s process file still stands: Art. 24 §2, three prior convention questions failed (1926/1950/1970), and 1994’s attempt to repeal the 20-year ask also failed.
